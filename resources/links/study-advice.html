Advice on learning algebraic geometry

学习代数几何的建议

Xinyi Yuan / 袁新意 · Chinese · Author advice · 2026-04-30; v2.2

A concrete route into Hartshorne-level algebraic geometry: prerequisite algebra, comparisons of Hartshorne, Vakil and Görtz–Wedhorn, exercise habits and topics that can wait. The author explains where to supplement a compressed presentation rather than treating a book list as a curriculum.

For students choosing a first scheme-theoretic course; compare the suggested depth of reading with your existing algebra background.

Difficulty ratings and textbook preferences are the author's judgments.

Why I chose algebraic geometry and number theory

我为什么选择了代数几何和数论

Yongqi Liang / 梁永祺 · Chinese · Personal learning account · 2012-12-16

A personal account of entering algebraic geometry and number theory, introduced on the author's teaching page as a record of his studies and reading. The accompanying teaching page also discusses exploring modern mathematics and choosing a research direction and advisor.

Read as one mathematician's route into a field, alongside the more systematic preparation advice on his teaching page.

An autobiographical account, not a current admissions guide; the older article uses a legacy web layout.

Checked 2026-10-16 · Source / author page

For potential Ph.D. students

Ravi Vakil · English · Author advice

Advice on learning from seminars, working out small examples, talking with other students and developing mathematical questions. The seminar section explains how to benefit from a talk before you can follow all of its technical details.

For advanced undergraduates and beginning graduate students learning how research differs from completing a course.

The opening supervision expectations describe this author's advising style, not a universal doctoral admission threshold or a current vacancy.

Checked 2026-10-16

How to work out proofs in Analysis I

Timothy Gowers · English · Worked learning advice · 2014-02-03

Shows how elementary analysis proofs can be built from smaller moves: expanding definitions, extracting useful hypotheses and rewriting the goal. A worked sequence argument makes the gap between recognizing a proof and finding one more tangible.

For a first proof-based analysis course; try the proof moves on an exercise before returning to the explanation.

Checked 2026-10-16

Learn and relearn your field

Terence Tao · English · Author advice

A short reflection on continuing to learn familiar material, including basic ideas within one's own specialty. It encourages revisiting solutions and connections rather than treating a solved problem as exhausted.

For deciding what it means to revisit a course after your mathematical perspective has changed.

Checked 2026-10-16

Advice to a Young Mathematician

Michael Atiyah; Béla Bollobás; Alain Connes; Dusa McDuff; Peter Sarnak · English · Collected essays · 2008; The Princeton Companion to Mathematics, VIII.6

Five perspectives on mathematical taste, problems and theory, reading, setbacks and research life. The contributions need not agree: read the differences as evidence that mathematical development does not follow one universal recipe.

Read section VIII.6; the PDF begins with the end of the preceding entry and continues beyond the advice essays.

Reflections on mathematical writing

数学写作漫谈

Wen-Wei Li / 李文威 · Chinese · Author advice / edited talk · 2015-04-24; based on a 2014 talk

Discusses mathematical writing as communicating ideas, not merely correcting English or formatting a paper. It treats readership, organization, exposition, examples, typography and talks, with practical comparisons of different writing styles.

For students writing reading reports, expository notes or a first thesis; useful to contributors preparing explanations for this site.

Checked 2026-10-16

Tips for Authors

J. S. Milne · English · Satirical writing advice

An intentionally sarcastic catalogue of bad writing practices, such as concealing definitions, omitting motivation and giving untraceable references. The joke works by making the reader's difficulties explicit.

Read the recommendations in reverse when editing your own mathematical exposition.

Satire: the literal instructions are deliberately bad advice.

Thoughts on graduate school

Noah Snyder; discussion by named commenters · English · Personal research advice & discussion · 2009-05-13

Discusses readable sources, mathematical narratives, taste, peer groups and finding an advisor. The comments include substantive disagreement about when to graduate and how to weigh publications, making this useful as a conversation rather than a checklist.

Read the comments as well as the post; the author explicitly frames the advice around his own graduate-school setting.

Historical personal advice; graduation, funding and employment conditions must not be inferred from this 2009 discussion.

Ten Lessons I Wish I Had Been Taught

Gian-Carlo Rota · English · Personal professional reflections · 1997

Reflections on giving lectures, communicating with an audience and habits within mathematical research. Rota's distinctive voice makes this a useful counterpoint to more procedural guides.

For students starting to give mathematical talks and participate in a research community.

Linked from Yongqi Liang's teaching page; this copy is hosted there, not on the original author's site.

How to read a mathematics book well

如何读好数学书？｜展卷

Jiu Ding / 丁玖 · Chinese · Authorized book excerpt / author advice

Explains how precise definitions, simple cases and the decisive idea of a proof turn reading into understanding. A request to negate an epsilon–delta definition illustrates the difference between repeating a definition and being able to reason with it.

For readers who recognize textbook arguments but cannot yet adapt them to a new example; an authorized excerpt from the chapter 读书妙法.

A published excerpt, not the full book; recollections of other mathematicians are presented through the author's account.

Checked 2026-10-16

Reflections on teaching and learning mathematical analysis

【论教】数学分析教学体会浅谈

Shihai Yang / 杨世海 · Chinese · Author-contributed university essay & discussion · 2016-09-09

Discusses independent reading, purposeful exercises and reflection in a first university analysis course. Comparisons of Cauchy–Schwarz proofs and the nested-interval proof of the intermediate value theorem show why an illuminating argument need not simply be the shortest one.

Read the student questions and the author's replies as well as the initial essay, especially when deciding how to practise analysis.

Reading, questioning and explaining mathematics

教师代表关启安教授在数学科学学院2021年开学典礼上的致辞

Qi'an Guan / 关启安 · Chinese · Faculty address · 2021 address

Connects repeated textbook reading with active questions: are a theorem's hypotheses needed, can its proof be simplified, and can the conclusion be strengthened? The address also discusses explaining arguments and learning through seminars.

A short starting point for moving beyond knowing a theorem's statement toward asking your own mathematical questions.

A student guide to undergraduate mathematics and further study

USTC基础数学修课指南（第三版第一稿）

USTC student contributors, including Junyan Zhang / 章俊彦 · Chinese · Unofficial collaborative student guide · 2020-09-26; third-edition draft

A 193-page student-written guide combining prerequisite planning, textbook discussion and explanations of different subjects. Selected sections discuss metric-space examples in topology and counting methods such as double counting, generating functions and the probabilistic method.

Use the contents to select a subject rather than treating the entire guide as a required linear reading plan.

Selected sections were reviewed, not every recommendation in the 193-page draft. It is an unofficial student guide, not a current USTC curriculum or admissions policy.

How to learn mathematics: motivation, content and connections

如何學習數學

Tim L · Chinese · Personal teaching advice · 2023-02-27

Organizes learning around a topic's motivation, its actual content and its connections to other ideas. Deriving the quotient rule through different routes illustrates how connections reduce dependence on memorized formulas.

For revising calculus or approaching a new subject; compare the explanation of why a method is needed with the method itself.

Learning to write proofs when you cannot judge your own

不確定自己寫的證明對不對，可以如何學習

Tim L · Chinese · Personal teaching advice · 2022-11-26

Treats proof writing as learning precise communication through practice, feedback and revision. The author compares revising a corrected proof with rewriting a marked composition, rather than checking only the final grade.

For a first proof course or reading group; a correct proof can differ from a model solution, but still needs its logical steps checked.

Routes into probability theory

要学概率论？（作者主页链接名）

Dayue Chen / 陈大岳 · Chinese · Faculty study roadmap

Outlines routes from undergraduate probability and real analysis toward measure-theoretic probability, stochastic processes and stochastic analysis. Separate sequences for continuing undergraduates and incoming graduate students make the preparation differences concrete.

Use the course sequence to locate gaps before advanced probability; this is a roadmap, not a sustained essay or a prerequisite for every probability course.

The display name follows the author's homepage link, not an independent article heading. Institutional and conference listings are historical; publication date is not stated.

Taking responsibility for learning at university

青年数学家刘思齐教授：从高中到大学的转变

Siqi Liu / 刘思齐; reprint published on Sohu · Chinese · Reprinted incoming-student address · 2021-09-11; reprint

Discusses taking responsibility for university study, comparing textbooks and writing and revising one's own mathematical notes. A useful suggestion is to prepare notes as though explaining the subject in a small textbook, then record where the lecturer's account differs.

Relevant to beginning undergraduates and independent learners organizing their first university-level courses.

The reprint was reviewed. Its claimed original Bilibili publication was not independently checked; this is not an author-hosted copy.

Understanding proofs without trying to memorize every detail

证明需要全部记住吗？（导读标题）

远方的学徒 (Zhihu display name) · Chinese · Zhihu personal learning advice · Updated 2024-12-22, as shown in Zhihu's English rendering

Reflects on an early ambition to memorize every theorem's proof, then focuses on reusable ideas instead. Sequence-limit examples show how pairing terms, adding and subtracting an expression, and the triangle inequality recur in analysis.

For students who understand an argument while reading but forget its details later; extract the proof's goal and reusable moves.

The display title is an editorial summary, not a verified Chinese question title. Author credentials were not independently established.
