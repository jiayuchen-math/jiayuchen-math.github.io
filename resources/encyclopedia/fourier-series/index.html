A Fourier series represents a periodic function through its coefficients against exponential frequencies.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Lebesgue Integral · Hilbert Space

Overview

A Fourier series decomposes periodic behavior into oscillations at integer frequencies. Its coefficients distinguish the contributions of those frequencies, just as coordinates distinguish components of a vector. The analogy becomes exact in the Hilbert space of square-integrable periodic functions.

Writing a formal series is not yet a convergence statement. Pointwise, uniform, and mean-square convergence have different requirements and can give different interpretations at a jump.

Prerequisites

Know complex exponentials, integration, and orthogonality. The exponentials satisfy eikx=cos⁡(kx)+isin⁡(kx)e^{ikx}=\cos(kx)+i\sin(kx)eikx=cos(kx)+isin(kx). For the mean-square theory, functions differing on a null set are identified, and an L2L^2L2 norm measures average squared error.

Intuition

A coefficient tests how strongly the function aligns with one pure oscillation. Orthogonality prevents different integer frequencies from interfering in an average inner product. An individual graph value can nevertheless be sensitive to how many oscillations have been retained.

Every symmetric partial sum with N≥2N\ge2N≥2 equals fff exactly.

A periodic function taking value one on (0,π)(0,\pi)(0,π) and zero on (−π,0)(-\pi,0)(−π,0) has jumps. The usual symmetric Fourier sums converge to one-half at those jumps under the piecewise smooth convergence theorem, regardless of how the function is assigned at the single endpoint. They cannot converge uniformly to this discontinuous function because every partial sum is continuous.

Important properties

For f∈L2f\in L^2f∈L2, symmetric partial sums converge in L2L^2L2, and Parseval's identity states

12π∫−ππ∣f∣2=∑k∈Z∣f^(k)∣2.\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|f|^2=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}|\widehat f(k)|^2.2π1​∫−ππ​∣f∣2=k∈Z∑​∣f​(k)∣2.

A proof first establishes density of trigonometric polynomials, using approximate identities, then applies orthogonal projection. The missing density step is what turns an orthogonal family into a complete coordinate system.

If fff is smooth and periodic, repeated integration by parts gives rapid coefficient decay and uniform convergence. Mere integrability is insufficient for a universal pointwise convergence claim. Averaging partial sums, as in Fejér summation, improves convergence behavior without changing the underlying coefficients.

Connections and further reading

Fourier transforms replace discrete frequencies by continuous ones on Euclidean space. Distribution theory extends Fourier series to singular periodic objects. Littlewood–Paley methods group frequencies by size to measure regularity. Salo, §§2.1–2.2 distinguishes the Hilbert-space and pointwise theories.
