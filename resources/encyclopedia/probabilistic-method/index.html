概率方法

Combinatorics · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

The probabilistic method proves existence by placing candidate combinatorial objects in a probability space and showing that a desired property has positive probability.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Finite Simple Graph · binomial coefficient

Reading routes: Counting, graph theory and additive methods

Overview

The probabilistic method proves that a combinatorial object exists by analyzing a randomly chosen candidate. Randomness belongs to the proof, not necessarily to the object eventually obtained. The method is particularly useful when a satisfactory explicit construction is difficult but unfavorable configurations can be counted.

Prerequisites

Use finite probability spaces, expectation, indicator variables, and elementary counting. Understand that linearity of expectation does not require independence. Independence is needed only when a calculation multiplies probabilities of events whose joint distribution has been justified.

Definition and conventions

Choose a probability distribution on a finite set Ω\OmegaΩ of candidate objects. If the desired event D⊆ΩD\subseteq\OmegaD⊆Ω satisfies P(D)>0\mathbb P(D)>0P(D)>0, at least one candidate has the desired property.

A common formulation counts bad configurations using a nonnegative integer random variable XXX. If EX<1\mathbb E X<1EX<1, then P(X=0)>0\mathbb P(X=0)>0P(X=0)>0: otherwise X≥1X\ge1X≥1 everywhere except possibly on probability-zero outcomes, forcing EX≥1\mathbb E X\ge1EX≥1. The strict inequality is essential. It is enough to use any distribution that supports the argument; uniform sampling is not required.

Intuition

Bad configurations can overlap heavily. Counting their expected number is often easier than evaluating the probability of each complete outcome. A favorable average rules out the possibility that every candidate is bad. This argument says that a good candidate exists without specifying where to find it efficiently.

Important properties

Writing XXX as a sum of indicator variables justifies the expectation formula even though indicators for overlapping cliques are dependent. The independent edge choices justify the probability for each fixed clique.

Another elementary use assigns each vertex of a finite mmm-edge simple graph independently and uniformly to one of two parts. Each edge crosses with probability 1/21/21/2, so some partition has at least m/2m/2m/2 crossing edges. Deleting the noncrossing edges yields a bipartite subgraph of that size.

Alteration extends the method: start with an almost suitable random object, then remove or modify a controlled number of obstructions. The modification must preserve the useful features already established.

Connections and further reading

The method connects Ramsey lower bounds, extremal constructions, and randomized algorithms. Existence alone supplies neither a success rate suitable for computation nor an efficient search algorithm. Keller and Trotter, §11.4 presents random clique avoidance; §11.6, Theorem 11.7 illustrates alteration.
