微积分基本定理

Analysis, Measure & PDE · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

For continuous integrands, differentiation recovers accumulation and antiderivatives evaluate definite integrals.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Derivative of a Real Function · Riemann integral

Overview

The fundamental theorem of calculus connects a local operation, differentiation, with a global operation, integration. Its two directions answer different questions: what is the rate of change of an accumulated quantity, and how can total accumulation be computed from a primitive? Keeping these directions distinct prevents a computational formula from being applied without its hypotheses.

The theorem explains why an integral can often be evaluated by finding a function whose derivative is the integrand. It does not assert that every useful integrand has an elementary primitive.

Prerequisites

Understand derivatives, continuous functions, and the Riemann integral on a compact interval. An antiderivative of fff is a differentiable function FFF satisfying F′=fF'=fF′=f. Two antiderivatives on an interval differ by a constant, a consequence of the mean value theorem.

Integration by parts follows by applying the theorem to a product of continuously differentiable functions. Substitution follows from the chain rule with the appropriate interval and orientation conditions.

Connections and further reading

Lebesgue integration replaces continuity by integrability in an almost-everywhere differentiation theorem. Recovering a function from its derivative requires absolute continuity, rather than merely an almost-everywhere derivative. Taylor, §4.2 proves the classical statements used here.
