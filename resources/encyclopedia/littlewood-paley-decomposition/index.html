Littlewood-Paley分解

Analysis, Measure & PDE · Research · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

Littlewood–Paley decomposition separates a distribution into smooth frequency bands to measure scale-dependent regularity.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Fourier Transform · tempered distribution · Lebesgue Lp Space

Overview

Littlewood–Paley decomposition groups Fourier frequencies into bands whose sizes grow geometrically. It provides a language for smoothness at different scales and a practical way to estimate functions outside the special Hilbert-space setting of L2L^2L2. Its research uses include nonlinear estimates and refined spaces such as Besov and Triebel–Lizorkin spaces.

The decomposition depends on chosen smooth cutoffs. The resulting pieces are not unique, but carefully constructed choices yield equivalent norms.

Prerequisites

Know Fourier transforms, tempered distributions, LpL^pLp norms, and convolution. Multiplication by a smooth frequency cutoff corresponds to convolution with a rapidly decreasing kernel. Orthogonality and Plancherel explain the square-function idea at exponent two.

Intuition

A band near frequency 2j2^j2j resolves spatial features near scale 2−j2^{-j}2−j. Differentiation costs roughly a factor 2j2^j2j within that band. Separating the scales makes such costs visible and lets one compare roughness by weighting the bands.

Connections and further reading

Frequency-band methods complement wavefront sets: bands record size, while cones additionally record direction. Fractional Sobolev regularity and nonlinear product estimates are natural continuations. Salo, §§3.3–3.4 states the inspected square-function results and explains which proofs are omitted.
