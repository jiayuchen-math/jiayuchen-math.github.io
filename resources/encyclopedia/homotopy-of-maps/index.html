Prerequisite concepts: Topological Space · product topology

Reading routes: Topology toward homology and homotopy

Overview

Homotopy is a continuous deformation from one map to another, with the domain and codomain fixed. It replaces literal equality by an equivalence sensitive to the topology of both spaces. A family of drawings may suggest a homotopy, but the mathematical requirement is joint continuity in the point being mapped and the deformation parameter.

Prerequisites

Understand continuous maps, products, and the interval I=[0,1]I=[0,1]I=[0,1]. Basic examples come from interpolation in vector spaces. For loops and fundamental groups, distinguish the map's domain from a chosen basepoint that must remain fixed throughout a deformation.

Definition and conventions

Continuous maps f,g:X→Yf,g:X\to Yf,g:X→Y are homotopic if there is a continuous H:X×I→YH:X\times I\to YH:X×I→Y with H(x,0)=f(x)H(x,0)=f(x)H(x,0)=f(x) and H(x,1)=g(x)H(x,1)=g(x)H(x,1)=g(x). We write f≃gf\simeq gf≃g. A homotopy relative to A⊆XA\subseteq XA⊆X satisfies H(a,t)=f(a)=g(a)H(a,t)=f(a)=g(a)H(a,t)=f(a)=g(a) for all a∈Aa\in Aa∈A and t∈It\in It∈I.

The unqualified definition here is unbased. A based homotopy fixes the basepoint. A map is null homotopic when it is homotopic to a constant map, with any additional relative or based requirement specified separately.

Intuition

Think of HHH as assigning a moving image to every point of the domain. Every point moves continuously, and nearby domain points must move compatibly. Separate continuity of all time slices is not sufficient. Requiring a homotopy relative to a subset adds fixed anchors; changing those anchors changes the problem.

Examples and non-examples

If YYY is a convex subset of Rm\mathbb R^mRm, then

H(x,t)=(1−t)f(x)+tg(x)H(x,t)=(1-t)f(x)+tg(x)H(x,t)=(1−t)f(x)+tg(x)

is a homotopy between any continuous f,g:X→Yf,g:X\to Yf,g:X→Y. Convexity ensures that each interpolation remains in YYY.

On the circle, the maps z↦zz\mapsto zz↦z and z↦z2z\mapsto z^2z↦z2 are not homotopic because their degrees are 111 and 222. Straight-line interpolation in the ambient complex plane is not a counterargument: at some points it passes through zero and so leaves the circle. By contrast, rotating the circle through an angle gives a homotopy of its identity to a rotation.

Important properties

Homotopy is an equivalence relation. A constant deformation gives reflexivity, reversing time gives symmetry, and concatenating deformations gives transitivity. Continuity of concatenation follows from the pasting lemma at the shared time slice.

Composing a homotopy on either side with continuous maps preserves it. Thus homotopy classes admit composition. Ordinary singular homology sends homotopic maps to the same homomorphism; a prism decomposition produces the chain homotopy establishing this. Based fundamental groups need based homotopies or an explicit basepoint-change comparison.

Connections and further reading

Homotopy equivalence compares spaces through maps whose composites are homotopic to identities. It is weaker than homeomorphism. Read Hatcher, Chapter 0 and §1.1 for the inspected definitions and the fixed-endpoint convention used for paths.
