巴拿赫空间

Analysis, Measure & PDE · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A Banach space is a normed vector space complete for the distance induced by its norm.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: normed vector space · Cauchy sequence

Overview

A Banach space is a setting in which approximation by a norm-Cauchy sequence produces an object inside the same space. It combines linear operations with a quantitative notion of error and a completeness guarantee. This framework allows functions, sequences, and operators to be treated as vectors.

Completeness is the decisive extra requirement. A normed space can support sensible finite calculations while still omit the limit of an approximation process.

Prerequisites

Understand vector spaces, norms, and Cauchy sequences. A norm is nonnegative, vanishes only at zero, scales by the absolute value of a scalar, and satisfies the triangle inequality. Its induced distance is d(x,y)=∥x−y∥d(x,y)=\|x-y\|d(x,y)=∥x−y∥.

Intuition

A Cauchy sequence expresses internal agreement among sufficiently accurate approximations. Completeness says that this agreement corresponds to an actual object rather than an absent ideal limit. In applications, one first proves successive errors are small and then invokes completeness to obtain a candidate solution.

Examples and non-examples

The continuous real functions on [0,1][0,1][0,1] form a Banach space with ∥f∥∞=sup⁡∣f∣\|f\|_\infty=\sup|f|∥f∥∞​=sup∣f∣. A uniformly Cauchy sequence converges pointwise because R\mathbb RR is complete; the common error estimates make convergence uniform, so the limit stays continuous.

The finite-support sequences with the ℓ2\ell^2ℓ2 norm are incomplete. Let x(N)x^{(N)}x(N) retain the first NNN coordinates of (1,1/2,1/3,…)(1,1/2,1/3,\ldots)(1,1/2,1/3,…). Completing it gives ℓ2\ell^2ℓ2.

Even continuous functions on [0,1][0,1][0,1] become incomplete under the L1L^1L1 norm: continuous ramps can approximate a jump indicator in integral error, with no continuous function representing that limit almost everywhere.

Important properties

Closed linear subspaces of Banach spaces are Banach with the inherited norm. Conversely, a complete linear subspace of a normed space is closed. Quotients by closed subspaces also admit complete quotient norms.

If YYY is Banach, bounded linear maps X→YX\to YX→Y form a Banach space in the operator norm. An absolutely norm-summable series converges: the triangle inequality makes its partial sums Cauchy, and completeness supplies the limit. This argument justifies Neumann series for I−TI-TI−T when ∥T∥<1\|T\|<1∥T∥<1.

The open mapping, closed graph, and uniform boundedness theorems require completeness of specified spaces. Removing those hypotheses can invalidate conclusions about inverse maps or operator bounds.

Connections and further reading

Hilbert spaces add an inner product, but many Banach norms do not arise from one. Weak topologies test convergence through linear measurements and can recover compactness missing in the norm topology. Erdman, §4.1 and Chapter 6 develops examples and the main completeness-based results.
