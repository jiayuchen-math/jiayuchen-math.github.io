Number Theory · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

The Dedekind zeta function counts integral ideals by norm, with an Euler product encoding prime splitting and a residue encoding the class number and units.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Number Field · ring of integers of a number field · Fractional Ideal of a Number Field · meromorphic function

Overview

The Dedekind zeta function replaces positive integers in the Riemann zeta series by nonzero integral ideals of a number field. Unique factorization of ideals gives an Euler product, even when elements fail to factor uniquely. Its local factors record how rational primes split, while its pole at 111 connects analytic behavior to class groups and units.

Prerequisites

Know number fields, rings of integers, ideal factorization and absolute ideal norms. Complex analysis is needed to distinguish the defining series from its meromorphic continuation. The class number formula additionally uses embeddings, roots of unity and the regulator of the unit group.

Definition and conventions

For a number field KKK, define, when Re⁡s>1\operatorname{Re}s>1Res>1,

ζK(s)=∑0≠a⊆OK(Na)−s=∏p(1−(Np)−s)−1,Na=∣OK/a∣.\zeta_K(s)=\sum_{0\ne\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathcal O_K}(N\mathfrak a)^{-s} =\prod_{\mathfrak p}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-s})^{-1}, \qquad N\mathfrak a=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak a|.ζK​(s)=0=a⊆OK​∑​(Na)−s=p∏​(1−(Np)−s)−1,Na=∣OK​/a∣.

The product runs over nonzero prime ideals. Both expressions converge absolutely in that half-plane. Powers use the real logarithm of the positive integer norm. Elsewhere, ζK\zeta_KζK​ means the continuation, not an unqualified evaluation of the original series.

The sum counts ideals, including nonprincipal ideals. Counting algebraic integers instead repeats generators differing by units and misses nonprincipal ideals entirely. Fractional ideals are also excluded: their norms need not be positive integers.

Intuition

Writing ζK(s)=∑m≥1aK(m)m−s\zeta_K(s)=\sum_{m\ge1}a_K(m)m^{-s}ζK​(s)=∑m≥1​aK​(m)m−s, the coefficient aK(m)a_K(m)aK​(m) counts ideals of norm mmm. Thus the zeta function packages an arithmetic counting problem. Multiplicativity reflects ideal factorization; grouping its factors above a rational prime reveals the residue degrees there. Ramification affects the local arithmetic, but its index does not appear as an extra exponent in the individual prime ideal factor.

Important properties

For every number field, ζK\zeta_KζK​ continues meromorphically to C\mathbb CC, with its only pole a simple pole at 111. With r1r_1r1​ real embeddings, r2r_2r2​ conjugate pairs of complex embeddings, class number hKh_KhK​, regulator RKR_KRK​, number of roots of unity wKw_KwK​ and discriminant DKD_KDK​,

Proof idea for the residue: partition ideals by class, describe each part using lattice elements modulo units, and count in a fundamental region for the logarithms of units. Every class has the same leading contribution. Partial summation converts the resulting linear counting asymptotic into a simple pole. Full continuation and the functional equation require additional analytic arguments.

Connections and further reading
