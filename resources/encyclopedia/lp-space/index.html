L^p空间

Analysis, Measure & PDE · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

An Lp space consists of measurable functions modulo almost-everywhere equality with finite p-integrability.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Lebesgue Integral

Reading routes: Integration, Fourier methods and weak solutions

Overview

The LpL^pLp spaces convert integrability into a geometry of functions. Their norms measure the size of a function and the error between two approximations. Different exponents emphasize different features: a large exponent penalizes high peaks strongly, while a small exponent gives more weight to total spread.

These spaces are built from equivalence classes. This is essential because integration cannot distinguish functions that differ only on a null set, whereas a norm must distinguish different vectors.

Prerequisites

Know measure spaces, measurable functions, and Lebesgue integration. Understand almost-everywhere equality and the role of a quotient: an element represents all measurable functions agreeing outside a null set. Hölder's inequality controls products, and Minkowski's inequality supplies the triangle inequality.

Intuition

The exponent selects how size is aggregated. Concentrating a fixed total amount into a narrower, taller spike can leave its L1L^1L1 norm unchanged while increasing its L2L^2L2 norm. Pointwise height and integral size therefore answer different approximation questions.

Important properties

For every measure space and 1≤p≤∞1\le p\le\infty1≤p≤∞, LpL^pLp is complete. A proof selects from a Cauchy sequence a subsequence with summable norm differences. Those differences produce an almost-everywhere convergent series, and integral estimates give convergence in norm; the full Cauchy sequence then follows.

On a finite measure space, Lq⊆LpL^q\subseteq L^pLq⊆Lp when q≥pq\ge pq≥p, by Hölder's inequality. No such unconditional inclusion holds on an infinite measure space. For p=2p=2p=2, integration defines an inner product, making L2L^2L2 a Hilbert space. Simple functions are dense, and on Euclidean domains smooth compactly supported functions are dense for finite ppp.
