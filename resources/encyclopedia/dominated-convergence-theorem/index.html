An integrable bound independent of the index upgrades almost-everywhere convergence to convergence in L1.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Lebesgue Integral · measurable real function · almost everywhere

Reading routes: Integration, Fourier methods and weak solutions

Overview

The dominated convergence theorem gives a practical criterion for exchanging a limit with a Lebesgue integral. Its central requirement is a single integrable function controlling every member of the sequence. Convergence at individual points then cannot conceal a fixed amount of mass in increasingly narrow peaks or in regions moving away to infinity.

The conclusion is stronger than convergence of signed integrals: the total absolute error tends to zero. This makes the theorem a bridge from pointwise information to the norm topology of a Banach space.

Prerequisites

Know measurable functions, null sets, and integrability in the sense ∫∣f∣ dμ<∞\int|f|\,d\mu<\infty∫∣f∣dμ<∞. Almost-everywhere convergence allows an exceptional measurable set of measure zero. No finiteness or sigma-finiteness of the ambient measure is required for the theorem stated here.

Intuition

The dominating function is an integrable budget for the whole sequence. A uniform numerical bound is a suitable budget on a finite-measure space, but generally not on an infinite-measure space. Likewise, a separate integrable bound for each nnn is useless unless these bounds can be replaced by a common one. The quantifier “one function for every index” does the essential work.
