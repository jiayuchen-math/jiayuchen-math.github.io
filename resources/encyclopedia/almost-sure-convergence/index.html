Almost sure convergence means pointwise convergence on one event of probability one and differs from other probabilistic limits.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Random Variable

Overview

Almost sure convergence states that a sequence of observations converges along almost every possible outcome. Its exceptional outcomes form one event of probability zero. This is a pathwise statement, stronger than saying that the probability of a large error becomes small at each individual time.

Probability theory uses several notions of convergence because experiments can be compared at different levels: individual paths, error probabilities, average errors, or distributions. Specifying the mode is part of a mathematical assertion.

Intuition

Almost sure convergence follows one outcome through all times. Convergence in probability looks across outcomes at each time. A small set of bad outcomes can move with time and repeatedly catch almost every path, preventing pathwise convergence despite vanishing error probabilities.

Average-error convergence additionally controls the sizes of rare mistakes. Very large errors on very small events may disappear on each path but still contribute appreciably to an expectation.

Important properties

Almost sure convergence implies convergence in probability by applying bounded convergence to the large-error indicators. Markov's inequality shows that LpL^pLp convergence implies convergence in probability. Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution.

Neither almost sure nor LpL^pLp convergence generally implies the other. If almost sure convergence has an integrable dominating bound for the relevant pppth powers, dominated convergence supplies LpL^pLp convergence. For p=1p=1p=1, uniform integrability is the precise additional condition accompanying convergence in probability.

Connections and further reading

Strong laws concern almost sure limits; central limit theorems concern distributional limits. Martingale convergence illustrates why an almost sure limit can lose expectation. Lawler, Section 4, Durrett, §§3.2 and 4.6, and Peking University, §1.4, Definition 1.14 give the precise modes and their conventions.
