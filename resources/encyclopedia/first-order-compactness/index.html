一阶逻辑紧致性定理

Foundations & Logic · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A set of first-order sentences has a model exactly when each of its finite subsets has a model.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Completeness of First-Order Logic · satisfiability

Overview

Compactness turns finite satisfiability into the existence of a single model satisfying an entire first-order theory. The models of the finite fragments may differ in size and interpretation. The theorem does not require them to fit into a previously chosen structure or to converge in an analytic sense.

Prerequisites

Understand models, sentences, and syntactic consistency. Know the soundness and completeness theorem for classical first-order logic. The background domain convention is nonempty, and languages and theories are sets. An infinite theory may contain infinitely many separate axioms, each individually a finite formula.

Definition and conventions

For a set Γ\GammaΓ of sentences in one fixed first-order language,

Γ has a model⟺every finite Γ0⊆Γ has a model.\Gamma\text{ has a model} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{every finite }\Gamma_0\subseteq\Gamma\text{ has a model}.Γ has a model⟺every finite Γ0​⊆Γ has a model.

The forward implication simply reuses the model of Γ\GammaΓ. The converse is the compactness theorem. “Finite subset” refers to finitely many premises, not finite models. A finite fragment may require an infinite model; compactness places no uniform size restriction on the witnesses.

Intuition

No contradiction can require infinitely many premises at once in a finitary calculus. If the whole theory were impossible, a finite portion would already reveal that impossibility. Compactness turns this observation about proofs into a theorem about structures.

Examples and non-examples

Expand the language of arithmetic by a constant ccc. Add to the first-order theory of the standard natural numbers all sentences c>nˉc>\bar nc>nˉ, one for each standard natural number nnn. Any finite fragment is satisfied in the standard structure by interpreting ccc larger than all the finitely many mentioned numerals. Compactness produces a model of the entire expanded theory.

Its ccc exceeds every standard numeral, so the model cannot be the standard natural numbers. The result supplies a nonstandard model, not a new standard natural number larger than all natural numbers.

For a contrasting requirement, insist on using the fixed standard structure for every sentence c>nˉc>\bar nc>nˉ. Each finite fragment admits an interpretation there, but the whole collection does not. The missing hypothesis is the freedom to choose a new model.

Important properties

Suppose every finite fragment is satisfiable but Γ\GammaΓ has no model. Completeness implies Γ⊢⊥\Gamma\vdash\botΓ⊢⊥. A finite proof mentions finitely many premises, yielding Γ0⊢⊥\Gamma_0\vdash\botΓ0​⊢⊥ for a finite Γ0\Gamma_0Γ0​. Soundness says Γ0\Gamma_0Γ0​ has no model, contradicting the assumption. This establishes the nontrivial direction.

If a theory has arbitrarily large finite models, adjoining sentences requiring at least nnn distinct elements yields an infinite model by the same argument. Consequently, the class of all finite structures cannot be axiomatized by a first-order theory.

Connections and further reading

Compactness is a central model-construction tool and also reveals limits of first-order expressive power. It differs from compactness of a topological space, although connections exist through spaces of theories. See Avigad, §7.2, Theorem 7.2.1 and Theorem 7.2.3 and Yang, PDF pages 22–25.
