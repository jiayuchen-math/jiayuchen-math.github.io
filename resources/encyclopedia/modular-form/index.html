模形式

Number Theory · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A holomorphic modular form obeys a weighted symmetry law on the upper half-plane and a regularity condition at every cusp.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Holomorphic Function · Group Action · Integer Congruence

Overview

Modular forms are highly constrained holomorphic functions carrying arithmetic information. Their symmetry is weighted rather than ordinary invariance, and their Fourier coefficients often encode counting or number-theoretic data. This article describes the classical holomorphic setting over the complex numbers; nonholomorphic automorphic forms and algebraic formulations require additional definitions.

Prerequisites

Use complex holomorphic functions, group actions and convergent series. Know that SL⁡2(Z)\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)SL2​(Z) acts on the upper half-plane H\mathbb HH by fractional linear transformations. Congruence subgroups permit additional level structure, but the full modular group suffices for the first definition.

Definition and conventions

For an integer k≥0k\ge0k≥0, a holomorphic modular form of weight kkk for the full modular group is a holomorphic function f:H→Cf:\mathbb H\to\mathbb Cf:H→C satisfying

for every matrix in SL⁡2(Z)\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)SL2​(Z), and holomorphic at the cusp. Since f(z+1)=f(z)f(z+1)=f(z)f(z+1)=f(z), write q=e2πizq=e^{2\pi iz}q=e2πiz; cusp regularity means its expansion has only nonnegative powers of qqq. It is a cusp form if its constant term is zero. For a finite-index subgroup, regularity must be checked at all its cusps in appropriate local coordinates.

Intuition

Changing a basis of a lattice gives different coordinates for the same underlying elliptic curve. The transformation factor compensates for that change. This is why a positive-weight form is more naturally a geometric section than an invariant function on a quotient. The cusp condition controls what happens when a lattice degenerates.

Examples and non-examples

Constant functions are weight-zero modular forms. A nonzero constant is not a weight-two form: the transformation z↦−1/zz\mapsto-1/zz↦−1/z would demand 1=z21=z^21=z2, which fails for general zzz.

The modular discriminant is the cusp form

Δ(z)=q∏n≥1(1−qn)24\Delta(z)=q\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{24}Δ(z)=qn≥1∏​(1−qn)24

of weight twelve. Its first term is qqq, so it vanishes at the cusp. The transformation law is an additional theorem, not a consequence of having a qqq-expansion alone. A generic convergent power series in qqq is periodic but is not modular.

Important properties

The matrix −I-I−I fixes zzz but contributes (−1)k(-1)^k(−1)k. Therefore a form of odd weight for the full group must vanish identically. This restriction need not hold for subgroups lacking −I-I−I or for nontrivial multiplier systems.

Products add weights. A quotient of two same-weight forms, with nonzero denominator, is an invariant meromorphic modular function, but may have poles where the denominator vanishes. Spaces of forms of fixed weight and level are finite-dimensional. Hecke operators act on them, turning arithmetic relations among Fourier coefficients into linear-algebra questions.

Connections and further reading

Modular curves explain the geometry, while L-functions package Fourier coefficients analytically. Read Milne, Chapter 4 for the precise cusp conditions and concrete constructions before studying Hecke eigenforms.
