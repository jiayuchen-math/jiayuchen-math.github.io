Fubini定理

Analysis, Measure & PDE · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

For an absolutely integrable function on a product of sigma-finite measure spaces, the product integral equals either iterated integral.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Lebesgue Integral · measurable real function · almost everywhere

Overview

Fubini's theorem turns an integral on a product space into repeated one-variable integrations. It justifies changing integration order for signed or complex functions when the total absolute integral is finite. The same principle includes sums, mixed sum-integral expressions, and many manipulations of convolution and Fourier transforms.

The central issue is cancellation. Separate one-variable integrations may hide positive and negative mass that cannot be combined into a finite Lebesgue integral on the product. Existence of both iterated integrals alone therefore does not justify equating them.

Prerequisites

Know measurable functions, absolute integrability, and sigma-finite measures. A measure is sigma-finite if the space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure. The product sigma-algebra A⊗B\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal BA⊗B is generated by measurable rectangles. For sigma-finite factors, the product measure is uniquely determined by its values μ(A)ν(B)\mu(A)\nu(B)μ(A)ν(B) on these rectangles.

Intuition

Integrating by slices amounts to assembling the same mass in a different order. Nonnegative mass cannot cancel, so approximation by increasing simple functions handles it. For signed functions, finite total absolute mass ensures that separating and reassembling positive and negative contributions does not create an undefined infinity minus infinity.

Important properties

A proof sketch establishes the slicing identity for indicators of measurable rectangles, extends it to measurable sets by a monotone-class argument, and then to nonnegative functions using simple approximation and monotone convergence. Applying this to positive and negative parts, or real and imaginary parts, proves the absolutely integrable case. Finiteness prevents undefined subtraction.

With counting measure on N\mathbb NN, the theorem permits exchanging two sums when ∑m,n∣amn∣<∞\sum_{m,n}|a_{mn}|<\infty∑m,n​∣amn​∣<∞. For convolution, f,g∈L1(Rn)f,g\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)f,g∈L1(Rn) give an absolute double integral bounded by ∥f∥1∥g∥1\|f\|_1\|g\|_1∥f∥1​∥g∥1​, justifying the relevant order changes.

Completed measures need care. The product of complete measure spaces need not itself be complete. For Lebesgue-measurable functions on Rm+n\mathbb R^{m+n}Rm+n, a completed-product version supplies the same almost-everywhere conclusions; one should not strengthen them to assertions about every section of every representative.

Connections and further reading

Fubini handles integration across variables, while dominated convergence handles a limiting operation inside an integral. Together they support parameter-dependent integrals, Fourier identities, and weak formulations. Hunter, §§5.4–5.7 gives the product-measure and completed Lebesgue formulations.
