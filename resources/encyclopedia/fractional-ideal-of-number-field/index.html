数域的分式理想

Number Theory · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

Nonzero fractional ideals extend integral ideals by allowing bounded denominators, forming a group whose quotient by principal ideals is the ideal class group.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Number Field · ring of integers of a number field · prime ideal

Reading routes: Arithmetic toward algebraic and analytic number theory

Overview

Integral ideals support multiplication, but their inverses usually leave the ring. Fractional ideals supply those inverses while retaining finite generation and a common denominator. They allow arithmetic statements about ideals to be expressed as group identities, even when elements do not factor uniquely.

The setting matters: throughout, KKK is a number field and A=OKA=\mathcal O_KA=OK​ is its full ring of integers. The group structure below uses the Dedekind property of AAA.

Prerequisites

Know modules over a commutative ring, ideals, fraction fields and prime ideals. Familiarity with localization explains the proof sketch. The field norm and the index of a subgroup are useful for the final connection, but they are different constructions.

Intuition

Think of a fractional ideal as an integral ideal after rescaling. Allowing denominators changes its position inside KKK, but does not discard its arithmetic structure. Prime ideals provide coordinates: multiplication adds their exponents, and inversion negates them. A denominator is therefore a controlled collection of negative prime exponents.

Important properties

Every nonzero fractional AAA-ideal has a unique factorization

I=∏ppvp(I),vp(I)∈Z,I=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(I)}, \qquad v_{\mathfrak p}(I)\in\mathbb Z,I=p∏​pvp​(I),vp​(I)∈Z,

with only finitely many nonzero exponents. It is integral exactly when all exponents are nonnegative. Consequently the fractional ideal group is free abelian on the nonzero prime ideals. Inclusion reverses coordinate size: I⊆JI\subseteq JI⊆J exactly when every exponent of III is at least the corresponding exponent of JJJ.

Proof sketch: localizing at each nonzero prime gives a discrete valuation ring, where every fractional ideal is generated by an integer power of a uniformizer. These local powers determine the original module; finite generation gives finite support. The inverse formula and uniqueness then follow from these coordinates.

Connections and further reading

Quotienting the fractional ideal group by principal fractional ideals gives the ideal class group. The Dedekind zeta function sums over nonzero integral ideals, whose norms are positive integers. Summing over all fractional ideals would introduce arbitrarily small norms and destroy that counting interpretation. Sutherland's Lecture 3 develops the underlying local arithmetic.
