A commutative ring supports addition, subtraction, and commutative multiplication with identity, without requiring division or cancellation.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: abelian group · unital ring

Reading routes: Algebra toward geometry and homological methods · From rings to sheaves and schemes

Overview

A commutative ring is an environment for polynomial arithmetic in which addition and multiplication behave much as they do for integers. Division is optional, and nonzero products can vanish. The concept therefore includes number systems, polynomial expressions, and functions with pointwise operations. Keeping these examples together makes it possible to study equations before choosing a particular kind of solution space.

Prerequisites

Understand abelian groups, binary operations, and homomorphisms. The additive group supplies subtraction. A unital ring additionally supplies multiplication and its identity. Ideals and modules are natural next steps, rather than prerequisites for the axioms themselves.

Intuition

The axioms permit expansion, factorization, and substitution. They do not justify cancelling a factor. For example, from ab=acab=acab=ac one obtains a(b−c)=0a(b-c)=0a(b−c)=0, but concluding b=cb=cb=c requires information about aaa. Likewise, writing 1/a1/a1/a requires that aaa be a unit. These are mathematical hypotheses, not merely notational choices.

Important properties

The units form an abelian group under multiplication. If u,vu,vu,v are units, then (uv)−1=v−1u−1(uv)^{-1}=v^{-1}u^{-1}(uv)−1=v−1u−1; associativity verifies both inverse identities. Addition need not preserve units, as 1+(−1)=01+(-1)=01+(−1)=0 in a nonzero ring.

If RRR is an integral domain, multiplication by any nonzero aaa is injective: ax=ayax=ayax=ay implies a(x−y)=0a(x-y)=0a(x−y)=0. This proof identifies the precise hypothesis missing in the modular example.

If a nonzero commutative kkk-algebra AAA is finite-dimensional over a field kkk and is an integral domain, then AAA is a field. Connections and further reading

Ideals specify which expressions become zero in a quotient; localization specifies which elements become invertible. These constructions lead to the prime spectrum and its scheme structure. Read Li's ring definitions and Milne, Section 1 for the algebra conventions used here.
