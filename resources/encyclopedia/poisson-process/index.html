泊松过程

Probability · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A homogeneous Poisson process counts arrivals in continuous time with stationary independent increments and a fixed positive rate.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: stochastic process · Poisson distribution · exponential distribution · independent random variables

Overview

A Poisson process models a succession of single arrivals at a constant probabilistic rate. Its output NtN_tNt​ is the number observed by time ttt. Specifying the distribution of each NtN_tNt​ separately is insufficient: the model also prescribes how counts in different intervals fit together.

This entry uses the homogeneous process with rate λ>0\lambda>0λ>0 on continuous time t≥0t\ge0t≥0. A rate that changes with time requires a different increment formula; a process with clustered arrivals may fail independent increments altogether.

Independence for increments concerns counts on disjoint intervals. It should not be confused with independence of the accumulated values NsN_sNs​ and NtN_tNt​.

Definition and conventions

A homogeneous Poisson process is a nondecreasing, integer-valued process (Nt)t≥0(N_t)_{t\ge0}(Nt​)t≥0​ with right-continuous paths, N0=0N_0=0N0​=0, independent increments, and

Nt−Ns∼Pois⁡(λ(t−s))(0≤s<t).N_t-N_s\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda(t-s)) \qquad(0\le s<t).Nt​−Ns​∼Pois(λ(t−s))(0≤s<t).

Independent increments means that for every deterministic partition 0=t0<t1<⋯<tm0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_m0=t0​<t1​<⋯<tm​, the counts Ntj−Ntj−1N_{t_j}-N_{t_{j-1}}Ntj​​−Ntj−1​​ are mutually independent. Their laws depend on interval length, which is stationarity of increments.

The right-continuous convention counts an arrival occurring exactly at ttt in NtN_tNt​. One concrete construction uses iid exponential waiting times WjW_jWj​ of rate λ\lambdaλ, arrival times Tn=∑j=1nWjT_n=\sum_{j=1}^n W_jTn​=∑j=1n​Wj​, and

Nt=max⁡{n≥0:Tn≤t},T0=0.N_t=\max\{n\ge0:T_n\le t\},\qquad T_0=0.Nt​=max{n≥0:Tn​≤t},T0​=0.

The arrival times tend to infinity almost surely, so counts are finite on bounded time intervals.

Intuition

The rate λ\lambdaλ measures expected arrivals per unit time, not fixed spacing between arrivals. Exponential waiting times allow both short gaps and long silences. Their memoryless property makes the future after a fixed time behave like a fresh counting process.

The construction makes the typical path a staircase with jumps of size one. It has stationary increments while NtN_tNt​ itself is not stationary: its mean grows with elapsed time.

Examples and non-examples

At rate λ=4\lambda=4λ=4 per hour, the chance of exactly two arrivals during a half-hour interval is

e−2222!=2e−2.e^{-2}\frac{2^2}{2!}=2e^{-2}.e−22!22​=2e−2.

The probability of no arrival during the next quarter-hour, conditional on the entire count history up to the present deterministic time, is e−1e^{-1}e−1. This follows from independent increments.

The process Nt=⌊4t⌋N_t=\lfloor4t\rfloorNt​=⌊4t⌋ has the same long-run average rate but is not Poisson: for a quarter-hour increment its count is deterministic rather than Poisson with mean one.

Batch arrivals also change the model. If MtM_tMt​ is a rate-one Poisson process and Bt=2MtB_t=2M_tBt​=2Mt​, then BBB has independent stationary increments but only even counts and jumps of size two. These increment properties alone do not establish the Poisson count law.
