A holomorphic function is complex differentiable throughout an open subset of the complex plane.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Continuous Function

Overview

Holomorphic functions are the central objects of one-variable complex analysis. Their defining derivative uses complex increments approaching zero from every direction. This requirement is rigid: differentiability on an open region forces local power-series expansions and derivatives of every order.

The resulting theory links differential equations, contour integrals, and approximation. A small amount of local derivative information controls much more of the function than it does in real-variable calculus.

Prerequisites

Know complex arithmetic, limits, and real partial derivatives. Write z=x+iyz=x+iyz=x+iy and a function as f=u+ivf=u+ivf=u+iv. Path integrals and power series are needed for the main consequences, though the definition itself uses only a limit.

Intuition

A complex derivative is one complex multiplier describing the same local change in every direction. Multiplication by a nonzero complex number rotates and scales the plane. General real linear maps can stretch different directions differently; most therefore cannot be complex derivatives.

Expanding its kernel geometrically gives the local power series and hence infinite differentiability. The identity theorem then says that two holomorphic functions on a connected domain agreeing on a set with an interior accumulation point agree everywhere. Connectedness and the location of the accumulation point matter.

Connections and further reading

Nonvanishing derivatives give local conformal maps. Isolated singularities lead to Laurent series and residues; holomorphicity on the whole plane defines entire functions. Taylor, §§1–2, 5, and 8 supplies the derivative, integral, and regularity viewpoints.
