谱序列

Algebra & Algebraic Geometry · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A spectral sequence is a sequence of differential objects whose successive pages are their cohomology, often computing associated graded pieces of a filtered cohomology group.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Chain Complex of Modules · homology of a module complex · quotient module

Overview

A spectral sequence packages a succession of cohomology computations. Its early pages usually involve simpler pieces of a problem, while later differentials record interactions between those pieces. When a convergence theorem applies, the surviving terms describe a filtration on the desired answer. This article uses the cohomological convention and focuses on spectral sequences arising from filtered complexes of modules.

Prerequisites

Understand chain complexes, their cochain reindexing, and homology as kernel modulo image. A decreasing filtration is a family of submodules ⋯⊇FpC⊇Fp+1C⊇⋯\cdots\supseteq F^pC\supseteq F^{p+1}C\supseteq\cdots⋯⊇FpC⊇Fp+1C⊇⋯. Its associated graded pieces are gr⁡pC=FpC/Fp+1C\operatorname{gr}^pC=F^pC/F^{p+1}CgrpC=FpC/Fp+1C. No derived-category formalism is needed for the construction described here.

Intuition

The first approximation sees only the part of the differential that stays at the same filtration level. A class surviving that approximation may still fail to be a genuine cocycle: its differential can lie deeper in the filtration. Later pages detect these increasingly distant failures. Taking cohomology discards classes killed by outgoing differentials and identifies those hit by incoming differentials.

A sequence of bigraded modules with arbitrarily selected square-zero maps is not automatically a spectral sequence: the next page must be identified with the preceding page's cohomology. A collection of ranks without those maps and identifications is insufficient.

Important properties

Suppose the filtration on each CnC^nCn is finite: for each nnn there are integers a(n),b(n)a(n),b(n)a(n),b(n) with Fa(n)Cn=CnF^{a(n)}C^n=C^nFa(n)Cn=Cn and Fb(n)Cn=0F^{b(n)}C^n=0Fb(n)Cn=0. Then the filtered-complex spectral sequence converges, with

E∞p,n−p≅FpHn(C)/Fp+1Hn(C),E_\infty^{p,n-p}\cong F^pH^n(C)/F^{p+1}H^n(C),E∞p,n−p​≅FpHn(C)/Fp+1Hn(C),

where FpHnF^pH^nFpHn is the image of Hn(FpC)→Hn(C)H^n(F^pC)\to H^n(C)Hn(FpC)→Hn(C). Proof idea: finiteness ensures that the successive conditions of being a cycle modulo deeper filtration eventually become actual cocycle conditions, and all relevant boundaries eventually appear. Unbounded filtrations require separate convergence arguments.

Even after convergence, graded pieces need not determine the target module. The abelian groups Z/4\mathbb Z/4Z/4 and (Z/2)2(\mathbb Z/2)^2(Z/2)2 both admit filtrations with two successive quotients Z/2\mathbb Z/2Z/2, although the groups are not isomorphic. Such extension information survives beyond the page calculation.

Connections and further reading

Double complexes and compositions of derived functors produce important spectral sequences used in Ext and sheaf cohomology. Read Li's general definitions alongside Stacks, Section 12.24, keeping the finite-filtration hypothesis in view.
