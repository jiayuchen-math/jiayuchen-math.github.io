Continuity expresses local control of output error by sufficiently small input error.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: limit of a real sequence

Overview

Continuity is the first precise bridge between pictures of functions and arguments about limits. It asserts that nearby inputs produce nearby outputs at a specified point, with a tolerance chosen to match the desired accuracy. The statement concerns the function together with its domain: the same formula can behave differently when the allowed inputs change.

Continuity neither requires a tangent nor bounds the speed of change. Its value is that it allows limits to pass through a function, turning information about an approximating sequence into information about its image.

Prerequisites

Understand absolute value, open balls, and the order of quantifiers in an error estimate. A metric ddd measures distance and satisfies the triangle inequality. On the real line it is d(x,y)=∣x−y∣d(x,y)=|x-y|d(x,y)=∣x−y∣. Sequence limits provide a useful alternative formulation.

Intuition

Think of ε\varepsilonε as an allowed output error. Continuity promises an input accuracy sufficient to meet it. It does not promise a universal calibration across the domain. A continuous graph may have a corner or extremely steep stretches; drawing without lifting a pencil is therefore only a limited mnemonic, especially for disconnected domains.

Important properties

For maps between metric spaces, continuity is equivalent to preservation of convergent sequences. To prove the converse, if the error condition fails, choose xnx_nxn​ within 1/n1/n1/n of aaa whose output error stays above one fixed positive threshold. This contradicts sequence preservation.

Continuous images of compact sets are compact. Consequently a real continuous function on a nonempty compact domain attains its maximum and minimum. Connectedness supplies the intermediate value property; a general disconnected domain does not. Sums, products, and compositions preserve continuity wherever the operations are defined.

Connections and further reading
