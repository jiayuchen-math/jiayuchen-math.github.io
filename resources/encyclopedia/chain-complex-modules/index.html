模的链复形

Algebra & Algebraic Geometry · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A chain complex is a sequence of modules with degree-lowering differentials whose consecutive composites vanish, allowing homology to measure failure of exactness.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Left Module · module homomorphism · quotient module

Reading routes: Algebra toward geometry and homological methods

Overview

A chain complex organizes successive linear constraints. Its defining equation says that every output of one differential automatically satisfies the next constraint. Homology then asks whether all solutions arise in this way. The same pattern governs relations among module generators and boundaries of geometric chains. This entry uses modules so that kernels, images, and quotient objects can be computed explicitly.

Prerequisites

Know modules over a unital ring, module homomorphisms, submodules, and quotients. Kernels represent solutions of homogeneous equations; images represent attainable outputs. Short exact sequences are useful for understanding the main structural theorem, but are not needed merely to specify a complex.

Intuition

A cycle is an admissible candidate for a homology class. Adding a boundary changes its representative without changing its class. Thus homology measures solutions modulo those already explained by a previous stage. It is a quotient module, rather than merely a count of dimensions, which matters when torsion is present.

Take C1=C0=ZC_1=C_0=\mathbb ZC1​=C0​=Z, d1d_1d1​ multiplication by 666, and all other terms zero. The chain condition holds because neighboring differentials are zero. Since multiplication by 666 is injective,

Adding zeros at both ends makes the latter sequence acyclic in all degrees.

Important properties

A chain map preserves cycles and boundaries and therefore induces Hn(f):Hn(C)→Hn(D)H_n(f):H_n(C)\to H_n(D)Hn​(f):Hn​(C)→Hn​(D). A quasi-isomorphism is a chain map inducing isomorphisms in every homology degree; it need not be an isomorphism on individual modules.

Maps f,g:C→Df,g:C\to Df,g:C→D are chain homotopic if there are maps hn:Cn→Dn+1h_n:C_n\to D_{n+1}hn​:Cn​→Dn+1​ with

fn−gn=dn+1Dhn+hn−1dnC.f_n-g_n=d^D_{n+1}h_n+h_{n-1}d^C_n.fn​−gn​=dn+1D​hn​+hn−1​dnC​.

For a cycle zzz, the last summand vanishes, leaving a boundary. This proves that homotopic maps induce the same homology map. In particular a chain homotopy equivalence is a quasi-isomorphism.

A degreewise short exact sequence of complexes 0→A→B→C→00\to A\to B\to C\to00→A→B→C→0 yields a long exact sequence with connecting maps Hn(C)→Hn−1(A)H_n(C)\to H_{n-1}(A)Hn​(C)→Hn−1​(A). Proof idea: lift a cycle of CCC to BBB, apply its differential, and use exactness to identify the result with an element of AAA. The square-zero equation makes it a cycle; changing the lift alters its class only by a boundary. This construction explains the one-degree decrease.

Connections and further reading

Projective resolutions are exact augmented complexes used to compute Ext. Filtered complexes organize successive homology calculations into spectral sequences. For the conventions and theorems above, see Stacks, Section 12.13 and Li's chain/cochain comparison.
