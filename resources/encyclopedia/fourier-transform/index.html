The Fourier transform represents a function by continuous frequencies and converts differentiation into multiplication.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Lebesgue Integral · Lebesgue Lp Space

Reading routes: Integration, Fourier methods and weak solutions

Overview

The Fourier transform studies functions on Euclidean space through their responses to oscillations. Unlike a Fourier series, whose frequencies form an integer lattice, it has a continuous frequency variable. It is useful both as an integral formula for integrable functions and as an operator on Hilbert spaces or distributions. These settings share compatible formulas but require different interpretations of convergence.

For differential equations, its central advantage is that a spatial derivative becomes multiplication by frequency. A differential equation can therefore become a family of algebraic equations or ordinary differential equations indexed by frequency.

Prerequisites

Know the Lebesgue integral, complex exponentials, and the distinction between L1L^1L1 and L2L^2L2. Absolute integrability justifies the initial integral; completeness of L2L^2L2 supports its later extension. Schwartz functions are smooth functions whose derivatives decay faster than every inverse polynomial, providing a convenient class on which all the basic operations behave well.

Intuition

Multiplication by e−ix⋅ξe^{-ix\cdot\xi}e−ix⋅ξ tests how much a function aligns with an oscillation of frequency ξ\xiξ. Cancellation makes many high-frequency measurements small. This is a useful picture, although the oscillations themselves are not L2(Rn)L^2(\mathbb R^n)L2(Rn) vectors: the transform is not literally a countable expansion in a Hilbert basis of plane waves.
