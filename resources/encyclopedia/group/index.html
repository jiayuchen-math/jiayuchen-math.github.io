A group is a set with an associative operation, an identity, and inverses for every element.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: monoid

Reading routes: Algebra toward geometry and homological methods

Overview

A group records operations that can be composed and undone. Its elements might be integers, invertible matrices, or symmetries of a figure. The operation belongs to the data: the integers under addition form a group, whereas the same set under ordinary multiplication does not. Understanding that distinction prevents treating a familiar set as an algebraic structure before specifying its operation.

Prerequisites

Use sets, functions, and composition of functions. A binary operation on GGG is a function G×G→GG\times G\to GG×G→G, so closure is already built into this notation. A monoid has an associative operation and an identity; a group adds invertibility of every element.

Definition and conventions

and each aaa has an element a−1a^{-1}a−1 satisfying aa−1=a−1a=eaa^{-1}=a^{-1}a=eaa−1=a−1a=e. These axioms force the identity and each inverse to be unique. Commutativity is an additional condition, ab=baab=baab=ba; a group satisfying it is abelian. Additive notation uses 000 and −a-a−a in place of eee and a−1a^{-1}a−1. For permutations, this article composes from right to left.

Intuition

Think of an element as a reversible instruction. Associativity says that batching consecutive instructions does not change the result. It says nothing about exchanging their order. Reversibility is the decisive difference between a group and a general collection of operations: a projection loses information, but a rotation can be undone.

Examples and non-examples

In (Z,+)(\mathbb Z,+)(Z,+), the inverse of 777 is −7-7−7. In (Z/5Z,+)(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z,+)(Z/5Z,+), the inverse of the class of 222 is the class of 333, since 2+3≡0(mod5)2+3\equiv0\pmod52+3≡0(mod5).

The permutations of three letters give a nonabelian example. With the convention above, (12)(23)(12)(23)(12)(23) sends 111 to 222, whereas (23)(12)(23)(12)(23)(12) sends 111 to 333. Thus these operations do not commute. Nonnegative integers under addition form a monoid but not a group: 111 has no additive inverse within that set. All square matrices under multiplication also fail the inverse axiom because the zero matrix is singular.

Important properties

Cancellation follows from inverses. If ab=acab=acab=ac, multiplying on the left by a−1a^{-1}a−1 gives b=cb=cb=c. Likewise right cancellation holds. The inverse of a product reverses the order:

(ab)−1=b−1a−1.(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}.(ab)−1=b−1a−1.

Indeed, multiplying either way gives the identity. The reversal matters in nonabelian groups. For finite groups, cosets of a subgroup partition the group into equal-sized pieces; consequently the subgroup's order divides the group's order. Finiteness is essential to that numerical statement.

Connections and further reading

Group actions turn this abstract operation into actual transformations of sets. Normal subgroups permit quotient groups, and representations turn group elements into invertible linear maps. Read Milne, Chapter 1 for the foundational definitions and permutation examples before proceeding to these constructions.
