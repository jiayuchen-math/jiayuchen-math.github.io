中心极限定理

Probability · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

For iid observations with finite positive variance, the centered sum divided by its standard deviation converges in distribution to a standard normal law.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: independent and identically distributed · variance · Convergence in Distribution · normal distribution

Reading routes: Probability toward martingales and stochastic processes

Overview

The central limit theorem describes the distribution of fluctuations in a large sum. It explains why normal approximations can arise from observations that are individually discrete or asymmetric. The approximation concerns a centered and rescaled sum, not a claim that the original observations become normal.

There are many central limit theorems. Here the scope is the classical iid theorem for real variables with finite positive variance. Dependence, unequal distributions, and infinite variance require separate hypotheses and sometimes different limits.

Intuition

Centering removes the predictable linear growth, while division by n\sqrt nn​ keeps the remaining variance fixed. Without this scaling, a sample average collapses toward its mean rather than retaining a nondegenerate limiting shape.

At the transform level, finite variance gives every centered, standardized summand the same leading quadratic behavior near the origin. Independence turns the transform of the sum into a product; the quadratic term survives the limit while the smaller remainder disappears.

The characteristic-function continuity theorem identifies the limit as standard normal. Finite second moment justifies the expansion; an infinite power-series expansion of all moments is unnecessary.

The CLT and the law of large numbers answer different questions: stabilization of an average versus the limiting distribution of its magnified error. Neither theorem guarantees that relative errors in extremely small tail probabilities are small for a chosen finite nnn.

Connections and further reading

Characteristic functions provide the main proof mechanism. The binomial specialization is the de Moivre–Laplace limit. Lawler, Proposition 5.4 and Theorem 5.5 give the proof, while Li, §4.6.2 documents 中心极限定理. Durrett, Example 3.3.16 supplies the Cauchy comparison.
