紧自伴算子的谱定理

Analysis, Measure & PDE · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A compact self-adjoint operator on a separable Hilbert space has an orthonormal eigenbasis, with nonzero eigenvalues of finite multiplicity accumulating only at zero.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Hilbert Space · Compact Linear Operator · bounded linear operator

Overview

The compact self-adjoint spectral theorem extends orthogonal diagonalization of real symmetric or complex Hermitian matrices. It replaces a matrix's finite list of orthogonal eigenvectors by a possibly infinite orthonormal family, with convergence understood in the Hilbert norm.

The qualifications matter. Compactness controls the size and multiplicity of nonzero spectral values; self-adjointness gives real eigenvalues and orthogonality. The theorem here is not the general spectral theorem for arbitrary bounded or unbounded self-adjoint operators, which can involve continuous spectrum and spectral measures.

Prerequisites

Know Hilbert spaces, orthonormal expansions, and compact operators. Take the inner product linear in its first argument. A bounded operator is self-adjoint when ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,Ty⟩\langle Tx,y\rangle=\langle x,Ty\rangle⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,Ty⟩ for all x,yx,yx,y. An eigenvector is nonzero, and its eigenvalue satisfies Te=λeTe=\lambda eTe=λe. The spectrum uses failure of a bounded inverse, a condition broader than existence of an eigenvector.

Intuition

The operator stretches mutually perpendicular directions independently. Compactness forces the stretches on infinitely many orthogonal directions to become small: otherwise their images would remain separated and could not contain a convergent subsequence. Directions in the kernel are stretched by zero and may account for a large part of the space.

Important properties

A proof sketch starts by finding an eigenvector for a nonzero eigenvalue of maximal absolute value. Compactness supplies the limiting vector needed in the extremal argument. The orthogonal complement of that eigenvector is invariant under TTT by self-adjointness, so the construction can be repeated there.

If the process continues indefinitely, compactness forces the selected eigenvalues to tend to zero. The norm of the remaining restriction tends to zero; otherwise another nontrivial extremal direction remains. This proves completeness after adding the kernel. Likewise, an infinite orthonormal family in a nonzero eigenspace would have images separated by 2∣λ∣\sqrt2|\lambda|2​∣λ∣, contradicting compactness.

For an infinite list ordered by ∣λ1∣≥∣λ2∣≥⋯|\lambda_1|\ge|\lambda_2|\ge\cdots∣λ1​∣≥∣λ2​∣≥⋯, the spectral truncation satisfies

∥T−∑j=1Nλj⟨ ⋅ ,ej⟩ej∥=∣λN+1∣.\left\|T-\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j\langle\,\cdot\,,e_j\rangle e_j\right\| =|\lambda_{N+1}|.​T−j=1∑N​λj​⟨⋅,ej​⟩ej​​=∣λN+1​∣.

This is a uniform approximation over the unit ball, stronger than convergence for each fixed input. For a finite list, retaining all nonzero eigenvalues gives the operator exactly.

Connections and further reading

Eigenvalues of the inverse tending to zero correspond to Laplacian eigenvalues tending to infinity; the differential operator itself is unbounded.
