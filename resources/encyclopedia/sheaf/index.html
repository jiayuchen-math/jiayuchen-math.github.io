Algebra & Algebraic Geometry · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A sheaf assigns data to open sets with restriction maps so that compatible local sections glue uniquely.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Topological Space

Reading routes: From rings to sheaves and schemes

Overview

A sheaf describes mathematical data that can be specified locally and reconstructed uniquely when the local specifications agree. Continuous functions are the basic model: functions on overlapping open sets combine into one function precisely when their values match on the overlap. Sheaves extend this organization to algebraic data whose sections need not be ordinary functions with a single fixed codomain.

Prerequisites

Know topological spaces, open covers, and restriction of functions. The initial definition uses sets; groups, rings, and modules can be added afterward. A stalk uses germs, meaning that two sections are identified if they agree on some sufficiently small neighborhood of a chosen point.

Intuition

The axiom has two parts. Uniqueness says sections are determined locally. Existence says compatible local data are sufficient. A presheaf can satisfy one part and fail the other. In particular, a global restriction such as boundedness may obstruct gluing even when the data are genuine functions and local agreement is unambiguous.

Examples and non-examples

For any topological space XXX, let C(U)\mathcal C(U)C(U) be the continuous real-valued functions on UUU, with ordinary restrictions. Compatible functions define a function point by point. Its continuity follows because the inverse image of an open set is the union of its open inverse images on the covering sets. This proves both existence and, by pointwise equality, uniqueness.

Now let B(U)\mathcal B(U)B(U) consist only of bounded continuous real-valued functions. This is a presheaf, but generally not a sheaf. On X=RX=\mathbb RX=R, cover by Un=(−n,n)U_n=(-n,n)Un​=(−n,n) for positive integers nnn. Each fn(x)=xf_n(x)=xfn​(x)=x is bounded on UnU_nUn​, and the functions agree on overlaps. Their only possible glued function is f(x)=xf(x)=xf(x)=x, which is unbounded on R\mathbb RR. The failure is existence, despite uniqueness holding.

For a set AAA, the constant sheaf A‾\underline AA​ assigns locally constant maps U→AU\to AU→A. It does not require every section on a disconnected open set to have one global value. On two disjoint nonempty opens, independent constant values glue. By contrast, the presheaf assigning AAA to every nonempty open with identity restrictions, and a singleton to the empty open, fails gluing on a two-point discrete space when AAA has at least two elements.

Important properties

The stalk Fx\mathcal F_xFx​ consists of germs of sections near xxx, or equivalently the direct limit over open neighborhoods of xxx. Equality of germs is stronger than equality of point values: two continuous functions can agree at xxx without agreeing on any neighborhood.

A morphism φ:F→G\varphi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal Gφ:F→G is a family of maps on sections commuting with restrictions. For sheaves of sets on the same topological space, it is an isomorphism exactly when every stalk map φx\varphi_xφx​ is bijective. Proof sketch: stalk surjectivity supplies local lifts of any target section. Stalk injectivity makes those lifts agree after shrinking around each point, hence on overlaps by uniqueness. The sheaf axiom glues them into a unique inverse image. Merely having abstractly isomorphic stalk sets, without a compatible morphism, is not this criterion.

Connections and further reading
