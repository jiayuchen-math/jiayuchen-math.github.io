数域

Number Theory · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A number field is a finite extension of the rational numbers; its ring of integers carries arithmetic beyond ordinary integer factorization.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Vector Space · Integral Domain

Reading routes: Arithmetic toward algebraic and analytic number theory

Overview

A number field extends rational arithmetic by adjoining finitely many algebraic quantities. Linear algebra controls the size of the extension, while an associated ring of integers supplies its arithmetic. These are different structures: in the field every nonzero element is invertible, so factorization questions become meaningful only after specifying a suitable subring.

Prerequisites

Understand fields, polynomials, irreducibility and finite-dimensional vector spaces. For arithmetic, know rings, ideals and ordinary prime factorization. Galois theory helps study symmetries, but a number field need not be a Galois extension of the rationals.

Definition and conventions

A number field KKK is a field containing Q\mathbb QQ whose dimension [K:Q][K:\mathbb Q][K:Q] as a rational vector space is finite. Every element is algebraic over Q\mathbb QQ. The ring of integers OK\mathcal O_KOK​ consists of the elements satisfying a monic polynomial with integer coefficients. “Monic” is essential: satisfying just some nonzero integer polynomial defines an algebraic number, not necessarily an algebraic integer.

For α∈K\alpha\in Kα∈K, multiplication by α\alphaα is a rational linear map on KKK. Its trace and determinant are the field trace and norm. The norm here is multiplicative and may be negative; it is not a norm measuring vector length.

Intuition

An algebraic relation reduces all high powers of a generator to finitely many coordinates. Rational arithmetic becomes arithmetic in a finite basis. Integrality then singles out a lattice-like subring. The extra coordinates permit new factors and new units, but ordinary unique factorization may no longer survive.

Examples and non-examples

The field Q(3)\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)Q(3​) has basis 1,31,\sqrt31,3​ and degree two. Its nonzero element a+b3a+b\sqrt3a+b3​ has inverse

In Q(5)\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)Q(5​), the element (1+5)/2(1+\sqrt5)/2(1+5​)/2 satisfies x2−x−1=0x^2-x-1=0x2−x−1=0 and is integral, although it is not in Z[5]\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]Z[5​]. The field Q(t)\mathbb Q(t)Q(t) with transcendental ttt is not a number field, and neither is the infinite algebraic closure Q‾\overline{\mathbb Q}Q​.

Important properties

Characteristic zero makes every finite extension separable. There are [K:Q][K:\mathbb Q][K:Q] distinct embeddings into C\mathbb CC, and summing or multiplying their values gives trace or norm. The ring OK\mathcal O_KOK​ is a free abelian group of rank [K:Q][K:\mathbb Q][K:Q].

Its nonzero ideals factor uniquely into prime ideals, even when its elements do not factor uniquely. The ideal class group measures the obstruction to all ideals being principal. These results require the full ring of integers; a smaller order can behave differently.

Connections and further reading

Embeddings connect arithmetic to geometry, completions connect it to local fields, and class groups organize factorization defects. Milne's notes develop these transitions systematically.
