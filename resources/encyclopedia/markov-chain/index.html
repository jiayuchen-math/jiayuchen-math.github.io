马尔可夫链

Probability · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A finite-state Markov chain evolves by transition probabilities determined by its current state rather than its full observed history.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Conditional Probability · stochastic process

Reading routes: Probability toward martingales and stochastic processes

Overview

A Markov chain is a stochastic model whose present state contains the information from its past needed to predict the next step. This article treats discrete time and a finite state space, with transition probabilities that do not change with time.

The Markov property is conditional. Consecutive states can be strongly dependent. It also depends on what is recorded as the state: omitting a relevant component can make an observed process fail the property.

Prerequisites

Know conditional probability, finite probability distributions, and matrix multiplication. A stochastic process is an indexed family of random variables. Here its index is n=0,1,…n=0,1,\ldotsn=0,1,…, and its state space is a finite set SSS.

Use row vectors for distributions. This fixes the direction of matrix multiplication and prevents ambiguity in formulas for evolution and stationarity.

Intuition

The current state is a summary for prediction, not a statement that the past never influences the present. For instance, a machine's current operating mode can reflect its earlier history while still being sufficient to specify its next transition probabilities.

An iid finite-valued sequence is a special case, obtained when every row of PPP is the same distribution and the initial law agrees with it. Most transition matrices have different rows, so knowledge of the current state changes the next-state prediction.

Important properties

Multiplying one-step probabilities along a path and summing over intermediate states gives the Chapman–Kolmogorov identity. Hence the nnn-step matrix is PnP^nPn and the time-nnn law is αPn\alpha P^nαPn.

Every finite stochastic matrix has a stationary distribution. One existence proof averages α,αP,…,αPn−1\alpha,\alpha P,\ldots,\alpha P^{n-1}α,αP,…,αPn−1, takes a convergent subsequence in the probability simplex, and observes that multiplying the average by PPP changes it by (αPn−α)/n(\alpha P^n-\alpha)/n(αPn−α)/n.

If the finite chain is irreducible, every state can reach every other and the stationary distribution is unique and positive. If it is also aperiodic, the greatest common divisor of possible positive return times is one, and αPn→π\alpha P^n\to\piαPn→π for every initial law.

Proof idea for convergence: irreducibility and aperiodicity make some power of a finite transition matrix strictly positive. This permits coupling with a uniformly positive chance to make two copies meet in each block. Their remaining disagreement probability decays geometrically.
