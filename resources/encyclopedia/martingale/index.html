鞅

Probability · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A martingale is an integrable adapted process whose future conditional expectation equals its present value.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Conditional Expectation · filtration in probability

Reading routes: Probability toward martingales and stochastic processes

Overview

A martingale models a quantity whose current value is the best conditional prediction of its future value. Fair cumulative winnings and sequential estimates of a fixed unknown quantity are important examples. The definition always includes the information available at each time.

Martingale behavior is therefore relative to a filtration. Supplying future information can destroy it, even when the process itself is unchanged. Equal unconditional means alone do not establish the martingale property.

Prerequisites

One needs integrable random variables, conditional expectation, and a filtration (Fn)(\mathcal F_n)(Fn​), an increasing sequence of information sigma-algebras. Adaptedness means MnM_nMn​ is measurable with respect to the information at time nnn. Stopping times describe decisions that do not inspect future observations.

Intuition

The process need not stay near its current value. Its upward and downward future possibilities balance conditionally on everything already observed. This conditional balance supports arguments for dependent processes where independence of all observations would be unrealistic.

In estimation, Mn=E[Z∣Fn]M_n=E[Z\mid\mathcal F_n]Mn​=E[Z∣Fn​] updates an integrable target ZZZ. More observations change the estimate, but conditional on the present, the next estimate averages back to today's estimate.

Important properties

Expectations are constant, and integrable linear combinations with a common filtration remain martingales. Conditional Jensen's inequality makes integrable convex transforms submartingales.

Optional stopping at a bounded stopping time TTT gives E[MT]=E[M0]E[M_T]=E[M_0]E[MT​]=E[M0​]. One proof writes stopped gains as increments multiplied by indicators of having not yet stopped; each indicator is measurable before its increment. Passing to unbounded times needs additional hypotheses.

An L1L^1L1-bounded martingale converges almost surely, but convergence in L1L^1L1 generally requires uniform integrability. Constancy of the finite-time expectations does not automatically survive the limit.
