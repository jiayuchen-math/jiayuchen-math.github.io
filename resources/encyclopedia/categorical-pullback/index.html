A pullback combines two maps to the same target while requiring their images to agree.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Category · Categorical Product · Universal Property

Reading routes: Categorical language toward higher structures

Overview

A pullback, also called a fiber product, combines two objects over a common target. An ordinary product allows any pair of coordinates; a pullback permits only pairs with the same image in that target. This construction expresses fibers, intersections and changes of base in a common language.

The same name also appears in operations on functions and differential forms. Those are related constructions but are not definitions of the categorical universal property discussed here.

Prerequisites

Know categories, binary products and unique factorization. Set maps give the basic model. Topological examples need subspace and product topologies. In other categories, first identify which morphisms are permitted.

In sets the construction is

A×CB={(a,b)∈A×B:f(a)=g(b)}.A\times_C B=\{(a,b)\in A\times B:f(a)=g(b)\}.A×C​B={(a,b)∈A×B:f(a)=g(b)}.

This formula is a model in a particular category, not a replacement for the abstract definition.

Intuition

Imagine two descriptions that carry the same kind of label in CCC. The fiber product matches descriptions precisely when their labels agree. If one map describes a family over a parameter space, pulling back along another parameter map substitutes the new parameters and retains the corresponding fibers.

In contrast to an unrestricted product, a pullback can be empty even when both source objects are nonempty: their images may have no common label.

Important properties

Pullbacks are unique up to the unique isomorphism preserving both projections. When a terminal object 111 exists, A×1BA\times_1 BA×1​B is a product. Pullback along a composite can be performed in two stages: the two resulting objects classify exactly the same compatible tuples, giving a canonical isomorphism.

In topological spaces, use the indicated subset of A×BA\times BA×B with its subspace topology. The product universal property and the subspace criterion verify the required continuity. For smooth manifolds, the set-theoretic fiber product need not be a manifold; transversality is a standard sufficient hypothesis, not an automatic property of all smooth maps.

Connections and further reading

Pullbacks are limits of cospans. They organize base change of bundles and, in algebraic geometry, fiber products of schemes. The opposite construction is a pushout. Riehl, §3.1, develops the categorical definition and examples before more general results about limits.
