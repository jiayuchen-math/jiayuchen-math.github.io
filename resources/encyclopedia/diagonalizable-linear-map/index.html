可对角化线性映射

Algebra & Algebraic Geometry · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

An endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space is diagonalizable when the space has a basis of eigenvectors.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Linear Map · basis of a vector space

Overview

Diagonalization replaces a linear transformation with independent scalar multiplications along basis directions. It simplifies repeated application, polynomial expressions in an operator, and linear recurrences. It is a property over a specified field, and the presence of eigenvalues alone does not guarantee it.

Prerequisites

Use finite-dimensional vector spaces, bases, and linear endomorphisms. A nonzero vector vvv is an eigenvector of TTT with eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ when Tv=λvTv=\lambda vTv=λv. The zero vector is excluded from this definition because it satisfies the equation for every scalar.

Definition and conventions

An endomorphism T:V→VT:V\to VT:V→V of a finite-dimensional FFF-vector space is diagonalizable over FFF if some FFF-basis consists of eigenvectors. In matrix language, its matrix AAA satisfies

P−1AP=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn)P^{-1}AP=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)P−1AP=diag(λ1​,…,λn​)

for some invertible matrix PPP with entries in FFF. The columns of PPP are the eigenvectors in the original coordinates. Similarity, rather than arbitrary row reduction, is the relevant change of matrix.

Intuition

Eigenvectors identify directions preserved by the operator. A complete eigenbasis lets each vector be split into independently evolving components. Distinct eigenvalues supply independent directions, but a repeated eigenvalue can have too few eigenvectors to fill the space.

Examples and non-examples

Take

A=(2103).A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&3\end{pmatrix}.A=(20​13​).

Vectors (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) have eigenvalues 222 and 333. With PPP formed from these columns, P−1AP=diag⁡(2,3)P^{-1}AP=\operatorname{diag}(2,3)P−1AP=diag(2,3), so Am=Pdiag⁡(2m,3m)P−1A^m=P\operatorname{diag}(2^m,3^m)P^{-1}Am=Pdiag(2m,3m)P−1 for nonnegative integers mmm.

The matrix

J=(1101)J=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}J=(10​11​)

is not diagonalizable over any field: its eigenspace for 111 has dimension one although its characteristic polynomial is (x−1)2(x-1)^2(x−1)2. By contrast the identity matrix has the same characteristic polynomial and is diagonalizable. Repeated roots therefore do not decide the issue.

Rotation by a right angle on R2\mathbb R^2R2 has characteristic polynomial x2+1x^2+1x2+1. It is not diagonalizable over R\mathbb RR, but becomes diagonalizable over C\mathbb CC.

Important properties

Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are linearly independent. One proof applies T−λIT-\lambda IT−λI to a shortest supposed dependence, reducing its length and obtaining a contradiction.

More generally, TTT is diagonalizable exactly when its minimal polynomial is a product of distinct linear factors over FFF. Both splitting and absence of repeated factors matter. A self-adjoint real operator on a finite-dimensional inner product space is diagonalizable with an orthonormal eigenbasis, but arbitrary real operators do not satisfy this spectral theorem.

Connections and further reading

Diagonalization is the simplest case of decomposing a space as a module over F[x]F[x]F[x], where xxx acts by TTT. Jordan form describes failures when the characteristic polynomial splits. Read Hefferon, Chapter Five, Section II for the eigenvalue definitions and diagonalizability criterion.
