Noether 环

Algebra & Algebraic Geometry · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

A commutative ring is Noetherian when all its ideals are finitely generated, equivalently when every ascending ideal chain stabilizes.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Commutative Ring · Ideal of a Commutative Ring · finitely generated module

Reading routes: Algebra toward geometry and homological methods

Overview

The Noetherian condition places a finiteness requirement on ideals rather than on the number of ring elements. It ensures that a potentially infinite collection of algebraic constraints has finitely many ideal generators. This is a central reason polynomial algebra can be studied using finite presentations. The condition controls existence of finite descriptions; it does not supply an algorithm or a bound on the number of generators.

Prerequisites

Understand ideals, generated ideals, and ascending chains. A module is finitely generated if finitely many elements span it using ring coefficients. Ideals are submodules of the ring viewed as a module over itself. These observations connect the ring definition to its module version.

Definition and conventions

A commutative unital ring RRR is Noetherian if every ideal I⊆RI\subseteq RI⊆R is finitely generated. Equivalently, every ascending chain

I1⊆I2⊆I3⊆⋯I_1\subseteq I_2\subseteq I_3\subseteq\cdotsI1​⊆I2​⊆I3​⊆⋯

stabilizes: there is some NNN with In=INI_n=I_NIn​=IN​ for every n≥Nn\geq Nn≥N. The index NNN may depend on the chain; no uniform stabilization time is asserted.

More generally, an RRR-module is Noetherian if its submodules satisfy the ascending chain condition. For a commutative ring, RRR is Noetherian precisely when the module RRR is Noetherian. Noncommutative versions require specifying left or right ideals and are outside this entry.

Intuition

Imagine adjoining new equations one at a time and recording the ideals they generate. In a Noetherian ring, an ascending sequence of such ideals eventually stops acquiring new algebraic consequences. The generators need not be minimal or independent. Finite generation of the ring as an algebra over a specified base is a different condition from finite generation of each of its ideals.

Examples and non-examples

Every ideal in Z\mathbb ZZ is (n)(n)(n) for some n≥0n\geq0n≥0, so Z\mathbb ZZ is Noetherian. It nevertheless has infinitely many ideals. Any field is Noetherian because its only ideals are (0)(0)(0) and the whole field; this includes fields that are infinite sets.

For a field kkk, the ring k[x1,x2,…]k[x_1,x_2,\ldots]k[x1​,x2​,…] in countably many indeterminates is not Noetherian. The chain

(x1)⊊(x1,x2)⊊(x1,x2,x3)⊊⋯(x_1)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2)\subsetneq(x_1,x_2,x_3)\subsetneq\cdots(x1​)⊊(x1​,x2​)⊊(x1​,x2​,x3​)⊊⋯

is strict. To verify the nnnth strict inclusion, evaluate x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_nx1​,…,xn​ at zero. Every element of the preceding ideal becomes zero, while xn+1x_{n+1}xn+1​ remains a nonzero polynomial.

Noetherianity does not prevent descending chains. In Z\mathbb ZZ,

(2)⊋(4)⊋(8)⊋⋯(2)\supsetneq(4)\supsetneq(8)\supsetneq\cdots(2)⊋(4)⊋(8)⊋⋯

never stabilizes. A descending chain condition is the distinct Artinian condition.

Important properties

Here is a proof of the equivalence in the definition. If every ideal is finitely generated, the union I=⋃nInI=\bigcup_n I_nI=⋃n​In​ of an ascending chain is an ideal. Its finitely many generators all lie in one INI_NIN​, so I=INI=I_NI=IN​. Conversely, if an ideal were not finitely generated, choose an element outside each previously generated finite ideal. This recursively produces a strictly ascending chain, contradicting stabilization.

Hilbert's basis theorem states that if RRR is Noetherian, then R[x]R[x]R[x] is Noetherian. A proof organizes an ideal by leading coefficients in each polynomial degree, uses stabilization in RRR, and then subtracts matching leading terms to lower degree. Iteration and passage to quotients show that every finitely generated commutative RRR-algebra is Noetherian. The finite number of algebra generators is essential, as the infinite-variable example shows.

Quotients and localizations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian. Their ideals come from ideals upstairs, whose finite generating sets descend or localize. Also, every submodule of a finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring is finitely generated. Both the hypothesis on the base ring and the finite generation of the ambient module are needed for that statement.

Connections and further reading

The spectrum of a Noetherian ring is a Noetherian topological space: an ascending chain of ideals reverses to a descending chain of their zero sets. See Stacks, Section 10.31 for permanence results and Li, chain conditions for the module perspective.
