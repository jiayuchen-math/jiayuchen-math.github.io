模的张量积

Algebra & Algebraic Geometry · Graduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

The tensor product represents balanced bilinear maps from a right module and a left module.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Left Module · module homomorphism

Overview

The tensor product turns a compatible two-input operation into a one-input linear map. Its defining property is universal, rather than a prescription that every element is a single tensor. It appears in change of scalars, multilinear algebra, and the study of exactness.

Prerequisites

For a possibly noncommutative ring RRR, take a right RRR-module MMM and a left RRR-module NNN. Understand additive maps and quotient groups. Over a commutative ring, left modules may be treated as right modules by using the same scalar action.

Definition and conventions

The abelian group M⊗RNM\otimes_RNM⊗R​N has a map (m,n)↦m⊗n(m,n)\mapsto m\otimes n(m,n)↦m⊗n that is additive in each variable and satisfies the balancing relation

(mr)⊗n=m⊗(rn).(mr)\otimes n=m\otimes(rn).(mr)⊗n=m⊗(rn).

Every balanced biadditive map b:M×N→Ab:M\times N\to Ab:M×N→A, for an abelian group AAA, factors uniquely through an additive map M⊗RN→AM\otimes_RN\to AM⊗R​N→A.

It can be constructed as a free abelian group on pairs, divided by the additivity and balancing relations. If RRR is commutative, the tensor product is naturally an RRR-module. A general element is a finite sum of pure tensors, not necessarily one pure tensor.

Intuition

Balancing says that applying a scalar to one input is indistinguishable from applying it to the other. The quotient records precisely those identifications. The universal property lets one prove an identity by checking it on pure tensors, since pure tensors generate the group.

Examples and non-examples

For an ideal III in a commutative ring,

(R/I)⊗RN≅N/IN.(R/I)\otimes_RN\cong N/IN.(R/I)⊗R​N≅N/IN.

The map takes rˉ⊗n\bar r\otimes nrˉ⊗n to the class of rnrnrn. Balancing and the quotient relations verify that this is well-defined. Consequently

(Z/4Z)⊗Z(Z/6Z)≅Z/2Z.(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z) \cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.(Z/4Z)⊗Z​(Z/6Z)≅Z/2Z.

For finite-dimensional vector spaces with bases (vi)(v_i)(vi​) and (wj)(w_j)(wj​), the vectors vi⊗wjv_i\otimes w_jvi​⊗wj​ form a tensor basis. In two dimensions, v1⊗w1+v2⊗w2v_1\otimes w_1+v_2\otimes w_2v1​⊗w1​+v2​⊗w2​ is not a pure tensor: a pure tensor's coefficient matrix has rank at most one, while this sum has the identity coefficient matrix of rank two.

Important properties

Tensoring is right exact. It preserves a surjection and the description of its cokernel, but may destroy an injection. Tensor the injection Z→2Z\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb ZZ2​Z with Z/2Z\mathbb Z/2\mathbb ZZ/2Z: the resulting map is zero, hence not injective.

A module is flat precisely when tensoring with it preserves injections as well. Free modules are flat because tensoring with a free module forms a direct sum of copies of the input. Over a field every vector space is free, so tensoring is exact.

Connections and further reading

Tensor–Hom adjunction translates maps out of a tensor product into maps taking values in a Hom group. Tor measures the failure of left exactness. See Clark, Subsections 2.2 and 2.7 for the universal property and flatness.
