A symplectic manifold carries a closed nondegenerate differential two-form.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Smooth Manifold · Differential Form · exterior derivative

Reading routes: Manifolds, curvature and symplectic geometry

Overview

A symplectic manifold carries a closed nondegenerate differential two-form. This structure is the geometric setting for Hamiltonian mechanics and for global rigidity phenomena that cannot be inferred from volume alone. It measures a skew pairing of tangent directions rather than their lengths, so a symplectic form does not by itself supply a distance.

Prerequisites

Use smooth manifolds, alternating bilinear forms, differential forms, and exterior differentiation. Nondegeneracy is a pointwise linear-algebra condition, while closedness is a differential condition. Both matter, and neither should be silently inferred from the other.

Intuition

Nondegeneracy pairs every nonzero direction with some direction on which the form detects it. Closedness makes these local pairings fit a differential conservation law. The resulting Hamiltonian fields preserve the form. Choosing a compatible almost complex structure can produce a metric, but that choice is additional data.

Important properties

The form ωn/n!\omega^n/n!ωn/n! gives an orientation and volume form. A nonempty, positive-dimensional compact symplectic manifold without boundary cannot have an exact symplectic form: if ω=dλ\omega=d\lambdaω=dλ and n≥1n\ge1n≥1, then ωn=d(λ∧ωn−1)\omega^n=d(\lambda\wedge\omega^{n-1})ωn=d(λ∧ωn−1), and Stokes' theorem contradicts the positive total symplectic volume. The dimension qualification matters: a point with the zero two-form is symplectic under the definition above and that form is exact.

Darboux's theorem gives local coordinates in which ω=ω0\omega=\omega_0ω=ω0​. Its proof interpolates nearby forms and solves for a vector field whose flow compensates for the change. Consequently local symplectic geometry has no curvature invariant analogous to Riemannian curvature; global symplectic invariants remain highly nontrivial.

Connections and further reading

Lagrangian submanifolds and Hamiltonian flows lead to Floer theory. Moment maps and reduction impose symmetry constraints before taking a quotient. See Cannas da Silva, Chapters 1–2, 8, and 18 for the inspected form and sign conventions.
