The derivative is the coefficient of a first-order local linear approximation to a real function.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: limit of a real sequence · Continuous Function

Overview

The derivative measures a local rate of change through a limit of secant slopes. A finite derivative is more than a numerical slope: it gives a linear approximation whose relative error vanishes as the input increment shrinks. This interpretation explains why derivatives support both geometric reasoning and reliable small-change calculations.

A derivative is attached to a point and a domain. Differentiating a symbolic expression does not remove the need to check whether the relevant limit exists.

Intuition

Zooming near (a,f(a))(a,f(a))(a,f(a)) makes a differentiable graph increasingly resemble its tangent. The remainder criterion expresses this without relying on an image: after dividing the error by the input size, the error tends to zero. A corner has incompatible slopes from the two sides, so no single linear map describes it.

Important properties

Differentiability implies continuity because both the linear increment and the remainder vanish. Product and chain rules follow by expanding increments and controlling their remainders; the composition rule multiplies local linear approximations.

If fff is continuous on [a,b][a,b][a,b] and differentiable on (a,b)(a,b)(a,b), the mean value theorem gives some c∈(a,b)c\in(a,b)c∈(a,b) with f(b)−f(a)=f′(c)(b−a)f(b)-f(a)=f'(c)(b-a)f(b)−f(a)=f′(c)(b−a). Its proof subtracts the secant line and applies the interior-extremum derivative condition. A derivative bounded in absolute value by MMM therefore yields a Lipschitz estimate on an interval. Stationary points need not be extrema: x3x^3x3 has derivative zero at zero.

Connections and further reading

The fundamental theorem of calculus connects derivatives with accumulation. Weak derivatives extend differentiation through integration against test functions, allowing certain corners. Taylor, §4.1 supplies the local expansion and the mean value argument. In several variables the corresponding object is a linear map, requiring more than separate coordinate derivatives.
