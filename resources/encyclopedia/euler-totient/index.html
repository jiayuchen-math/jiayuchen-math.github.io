欧拉函数

Number Theory · Undergraduate · Mathematics Resources editorial compilation (AI-assisted)

Euler's totient counts invertible residue classes and connects factorization with modular exponentiation.

Chinese translation, usage & terminology

Prerequisite concepts: Integer Congruence · coprimality of integers · Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Overview

Euler's totient function measures how much multiplication modulo an integer remains invertible. It is an arithmetic function: its input is a positive integer, and its value reflects prime factors rather than numerical size alone. It connects the elementary act of counting coprime integers with a finite group and a useful exponentiation theorem.

Prerequisites

Use congruence, gcds, prime factorization and the criterion that a residue modulo nnn is invertible exactly when it is coprime to nnn. Understanding the Chinese remainder theorem explains the product formula, though direct counting also gives it.

Definition and conventions

For a positive integer nnn, define

φ(n)=#{a:1≤a≤n, gcd⁡(a,n)=1}.\varphi(n)=\#\{a:1\le a\le n,\ \gcd(a,n)=1\}.φ(n)=#{a:1≤a≤n, gcd(a,n)=1}.

For n>1n>1n>1, this is the order of (Z/nZ)×(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^\times(Z/nZ)×. The convention φ(1)=1\varphi(1)=1φ(1)=1 follows from the displayed count and makes multiplicative formulas uniform. For a prime ppp and integer r≥1r\ge1r≥1, there are pr−1p^{r-1}pr−1 multiples of ppp among the prp^rpr possible representatives, so φ(pr)=pr−pr−1\varphi(p^r)=p^r-p^{r-1}φ(pr)=pr−pr−1.

Intuition

The obstruction to invertibility is sharing a prime factor with the modulus. The totient counts the residue classes avoiding every such obstruction. This depends on which primes occur, not just on the number of classes. That is why a simple monotonic rule in nnn is not available.

Examples and non-examples

For modulus 181818, the invertible representatives are 1,5,7,11,13,171,5,7,11,13,171,5,7,11,13,17, giving φ(18)=6\varphi(18)=6φ(18)=6. For modulus 202020, the prime-factor formula gives 20(1−1/2)(1−1/5)=820(1-1/2)(1-1/5)=820(1−1/2)(1−1/5)=8.

The function is not monotone: φ(11)=10\varphi(11)=10φ(11)=10 but φ(12)=4\varphi(12)=4φ(12)=4. Nor is it multiplicative for arbitrary pairs: φ(4)=2\varphi(4)=2φ(4)=2, whereas φ(2)φ(2)=1\varphi(2)\varphi(2)=1φ(2)φ(2)=1. Coprimality is part of the multiplicativity statement, not an optional simplification.

Important properties

If gcd⁡(m,n)=1\gcd(m,n)=1gcd(m,n)=1, the Chinese remainder theorem identifies a class modulo mnmnmn with a pair of classes modulo m,nm,nm,n. A pair is invertible exactly when both components are invertible. Counting gives φ(mn)=φ(m)φ(n)\varphi(mn)=\varphi(m)\varphi(n)φ(mn)=φ(m)φ(n). Combining this with the prime-power calculation yields

φ(n)=n∏p∣n(1−1p),\varphi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac1p\right),φ(n)=np∣n∏​(1−p1​),

where the product is over distinct primes.

Euler's theorem says that if n≥2n\ge2n≥2 and gcd⁡(a,n)=1\gcd(a,n)=1gcd(a,n)=1, then aφ(n)≡1(modn)a^{\varphi(n)}\equiv1\pmod naφ(n)≡1(modn). It follows from the finite unit group, or by multiplying all units before and after their permutation by aaa. Without coprimality, the assertion fails: 24≢1(mod12)2^4\not\equiv1\pmod{12}24≡1(mod12).

The group size need not be its smallest common exponent. Modulo 888, the four units are 1,3,5,71,3,5,71,3,5,7, and each squares to one. Thus exponent two already works for all units, although φ(8)=4\varphi(8)=4φ(8)=4. Euler's theorem provides a valid exponent, not necessarily the smallest one.

Connections and further reading

The totient appears in cyclic groups, cyclotomic fields and Dirichlet characters. It counts units, not their smallest common exponent; those are different invariants. Conrad, Sections 1–3 provides an accessible bridge to Euler's theorem.
