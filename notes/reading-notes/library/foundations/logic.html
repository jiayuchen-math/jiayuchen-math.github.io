Studying Mathematical Logic & Computability

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Formal logic distinguishes a sentence being true in a structure from its having a derivation in a proof system. That distinction is the organizing question for this selection. Forall x supplies practice with syntax, truth tables and natural deduction; Sets, Logic, Computation turns attention to semantics, completeness, compactness and computation. If derivation rules still feel arbitrary, begin with the former before asking what a completeness theorem proves.

What to work on

  • Evaluate a first-order formula in a specified structure and assignment.
  • Distinguish semantic consequence from formal derivability.
  • Use compactness to construct a model satisfying infinitely many constraints.
  • Trace a Turing-machine computation and explain the halting-problem diagonal argument.

A starting text

Sets, Logic, Computation: An Open Introduction to Metalogic — Richard Zach, based on the Open Logic Project. Start with sections 7.2-7.5 on structures, satisfaction and variable assignments and 11.1-11.6 on natural deduction. Continue with 12.8-12.11 on completeness, compactness and Lowenheim-Skolem, then 14.3-14.4 and 15.4 on machines and halting.

Choosing what to read

Keep a small structure beside every quantified formula and evaluate it under explicit variable assignments. Write a derivation and a semantic explanation of the same consequence, marking where their methods differ. For compactness, spell out the enlarged language and check finite satisfiability rather than invoking the theorem by name. When reading the halting argument, identify the alleged decider's input and output before constructing the contradictory machine.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. Use compactness to show that any first-order theory with arbitrarily large finite models has an infinite model; write the additional sentences explicitly.

    A hint

    Expand the language by countably many constants and require them to be pairwise distinct. Check an arbitrary finite subset of the expanded theory.

    Work through the solution

    Let TTT be a first-order theory with equality, having finite models of unbounded cardinality. Add constants c0,c1,…c_0,c_1,\ldotsc0​,c1​,… and put T′=T∪{ci≠cj:i<j}T'=T\cup\{c_i\ne c_j:i<j\}T′=T∪{ci​=cj​:i<j}. Any finite subset Δ\DeltaΔ of T′T'T′ mentions only finitely many of these constants, say rrr of them. Choose a model of all of TTT with at least rrr elements and interpret those constants as distinct elements (the remaining constants may be interpreted arbitrarily). This expanded model satisfies Δ\DeltaΔ.

    By the compactness theorem, T′T'T′ has a model. Its interpretations of all cic_ici​ are pairwise distinct, so its universe is infinite. Forgetting the new constants gives an infinite model of TTT. The use of a model of all of TTT in the finite-subset argument also handles an infinite theory TTT.

    If this was the sticking point:

    • Proofs & Mathematical Writing — Review the quantifiers in finite satisfiability and the distinction between a finite subset and the whole theory.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the satisfaction and deduction review only if you can evaluate quantified formulas and give rule-by-rule derivations. Skip the whole selection only if you can also prove the compactness application and reconstruct the halting argument without conflating truth and provability.
