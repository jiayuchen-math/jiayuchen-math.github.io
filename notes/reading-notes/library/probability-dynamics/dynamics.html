Studying Dynamical Systems & Ergodic Theory

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Dynamics asks what iteration or time evolution does over many steps, even when an explicit solution is unavailable. Teschl studies flows, stability and discrete maps using analysis and differential equations. Bruin develops invariant measures, recurrence and ergodic behavior. Choose the smooth or discrete route for local stability and phase portraits; choose the measure-preserving route when the question concerns typical long-term behavior with respect to a specified measure.

What to work on

  • Describe a flow, its orbits and invariant sets.
  • Distinguish stability, attraction and asymptotic stability.
  • Use a Lyapunov function and state what its derivative proves.
  • Determine local stability of fixed points of a discrete map.

A starting text

Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems — Gerald Teschl. Read 6.1-6.3 on flows, orbits and invariant sets and 6.5-6.6 on stability and Lyapunov methods; use 9.1 for linear stability. For a discrete route read 10.1-10.2 and 10.4 on logistic maps, periodic points and local behavior.

Choosing what to read

Find fixed points and compute the derivative or linearization, then state when that local test is inconclusive. Compare a contracting flow with a rotation so that stability and attraction remain separate. For a discrete map, follow a few orbits but use an argument to justify any limiting claim. Before an ergodic theorem, identify the invariant measure, the observable and the mode of convergence. Revisit measure theory if almost-everywhere statements are being inferred from a picture.

  1. For x'=-x and y'=-2y, write the flow and use V=x squared+y squared to prove global asymptotic stability of the origin. Explain why V=x squared+y squared cannot prove attraction for x'=-y, y'=x.

    Differentiate V along each vector field. For stability give a delta that controls all future times; for attraction show decay from every initial point.

Already comfortable with this? Skip flow and stability review if you can prove the first check with the definitions of stability and attraction.
