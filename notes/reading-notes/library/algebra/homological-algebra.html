Studying Homological Algebra

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Homology measures cycles left over after boundaries have been removed, but mistakes often begin before that quotient is formed. Baker keeps complexes, connecting maps and homotopies close together. The homological sections of Etingof and collaborators give a continuation into resolutions and explicit Ext and Tor calculations. The Stacks Project supplies a detailed tagged reference when a construction's hypotheses need checking. Start with a connected argument, then choose calculations or reference work according to the gap.

What to work on

  • Compute homology from kernels and images in an explicit complex.
  • Construct a connecting map for a short exact sequence of complexes.
  • Build a free resolution of a cyclic module.
  • Compute first Ext and Tor groups from that resolution.

A starting text

Notes on Basic Homological Algebra — Andrew Baker. Read sections 1-3: Chain complexes and their homology; Short exact sequences of chain complexes; Chain homotopies and quasi-isomorphisms. In section 2 work through the connecting-map construction before proceeding to resolutions in the alternative text.

Choosing what to read

Compute a small complex of abelian groups, checking that consecutive differentials compose to zero. Construct a connecting map by lifting an element and prove independence from the chosen lift. Draw each square when comparing complexes, and distinguish a chain homotopy equivalence from a quasi-isomorphism. Only after those operations are reliable should resolutions become the main object of study. Keep module calculations alongside the categorical language so an exact sequence continues to describe actual kernels and images.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. Resolve Z/4Z by free Z-modules and compute Tor_1 over Z of (Z/4Z, Z/6Z) and Ext^1 over Z of (Z/4Z, Z/6Z), showing the kernel or cokernel used in each case.

    A hint

    Use multiplication by 4 in a length-one free resolution. Tensor gives a kernel; applying Hom gives a cokernel.

    Work through the solution

    The sequence 0→Z→×4Z→Z/4Z→00\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\times4}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\to00→Z×4​Z→Z/4Z→0 is exact: multiplication by 444 is injective and its image is the kernel of reduction modulo 444. It is a free resolution. Set M=Z/6ZM=\mathbb Z/6\mathbb ZM=Z/6Z. Tensoring the resolution with MMM gives the two-term chain complex M→×4MM\xrightarrow{\times4}MM×4​M in degrees 1,01,01,0. Thus

    Tor⁡1Z(Z/4Z,M)=ker⁡(4:M→M)={0ˉ,3ˉ}≅Z/2Z.\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,M)=\ker(4:M\to M)=\{\bar0,\bar3\}\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.Tor1Z​(Z/4Z,M)=ker(4:M→M)={0ˉ,3ˉ}≅Z/2Z.

    Applying Hom⁡Z(−,M)\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(-,M)HomZ​(−,M) gives the cochain complex M→×4MM\xrightarrow{\times4}MM×4​M in degrees 0,10,10,1. Its degree-one cohomology is M/4MM/4MM/4M. Here 4M={0ˉ,2ˉ,4ˉ}4M=\{\bar0,\bar2,\bar4\}4M={0ˉ,2ˉ,4ˉ}, so Ext⁡Z1(Z/4Z,M)≅Z/2Z\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,M)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb ZExtZ1​(Z/4Z,M)≅Z/2Z. The two answers are isomorphic, but arise from different operations: a kernel and a cokernel.

    If this was the sticking point:

    • Abstract Algebra & Galois Theory — Review cyclic modules, exactness and quotient groups.
    • Linear Algebra — Rehearse kernel/image computations before replacing vector spaces by modules.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the complex review if you can construct and check the connecting homomorphism, including independence of representatives. Skip the resolution selection only if you can compute both Ext and Tor in the cyclic example and justify independence of the chosen resolution.
