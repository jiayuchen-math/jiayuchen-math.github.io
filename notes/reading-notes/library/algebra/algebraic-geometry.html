Studying Algebraic Geometry

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Algebraic geometry becomes easier to navigate when equations, coordinate rings and local functions remain visible together. Milne starts with varieties over an algebraically closed field; Vakil develops schemes and makes sheaf language part of the working machinery. Choose according to the gap you need to close: geometric examples and regular maps, or the passage from rings to spaces that can retain nilpotents. Reading both from the beginning is unnecessary.

What to work on

  • Translate between affine algebraic sets and radical ideals over an algebraically closed field.
  • Compute coordinate rings and describe regular maps through ring homomorphisms.
  • Compute a Zariski tangent space and detect a singular point.
  • Explain what additional information a scheme's structure sheaf records.

A starting text

Algebraic Geometry — James S. Milne. Read chapter 2, sections a-e and h-j on algebraic sets, Nullstellensatz, irreducibility and regular maps. Then use chapter 4, sections a and d on tangent spaces. Read chapter 3, sections a-f only when ready for the sheaf description of affine varieties.

Choosing what to read

Start by translating the parabola and a singular plane curve between equations and coordinate rings. Compute a tangent space before accepting its abstract definition. In the scheme route, verify the sheaf axioms on continuous functions, then inspect distinguished opens in an affine spectrum. Keep localization and gluing calculations beside the definitions; return to commutative algebra when those calculations become formal gestures.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. Over C, compute the coordinate ring and irreducible components of V(xy) in affine two-space and identify its minimal prime ideals.

    A hint

    A product vanishes when at least one factor vanishes. For the algebra, use that every prime ideal containing xyxyxy contains xxx or yyy.

    Work through the solution

    The zero set is the union V(x)∪V(y)V(x)\cup V(y)V(x)∪V(y) of the two coordinate lines. Its coordinate ring is A=C[x,y]/(xy)A=\mathbb C[x,y]/(xy)A=C[x,y]/(xy): the ideal (xy)=(x)∩(y)(xy)=(x)\cap(y)(xy)=(x)∩(y) is radical. Each line is irreducible, since its coordinate ring is a polynomial ring in one variable over a field.

    The minimal prime ideals of AAA are (xˉ)(\bar x)(xˉ) and (yˉ)(\bar y)(yˉ​). Their quotients are respectively C[y]\mathbb C[y]C[y] and C[x]\mathbb C[x]C[x], so they are prime. Any prime containing xyxyxy before passing to the quotient contains one of x,yx,yx,y, which proves minimality and exhausts the possibilities.

    This ring has zero divisors, because xˉyˉ=0\bar x\bar y=0xˉyˉ​=0 with both factors nonzero, but it has no nonzero nilpotents. Reducibility and nonreducedness are different issues.

  2. Compute the Zariski tangent spaces of y squared = x cubed at (0,0) and (1,1), compare their dimensions with the curve dimension, and identify which point is singular.

    −3a2u+2bv=0.-3a^2u+2bv=0.−3a2u+2bv=0.

    At the origin, this imposes no linear condition, so T(0,0)X=C2T_{(0,0)}X=\mathbb C^2T(0,0)​X=C2 has dimension two. The curve itself has dimension one; the origin is singular.

    At (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), the condition is −3u+2v=0-3u+2v=0−3u+2v=0, hence T(1,1)X=span⁡((2,3))T_{(1,1)}X=\operatorname{span}((2,3))T(1,1)​X=span((2,3)) has dimension one, and the point is smooth. The corresponding affine tangent line in the drawing plane is y−1=32(x−1)y-1=\tfrac32(x-1)y−1=23​(x−1).

    At the cusp, the visible limiting tangent direction is horizontal, but the Zariski tangent space is the whole ambient plane. The latter is defined by the linear terms of the equations, not by choosing the line that best fits a sketch.

  3. Let k be a field and X=Spec(k[t]). On U=D(t) take s_U=t squared/t; on V=D(1-t) take s_V=(t-t squared)/(1-t). Prove U and V cover X, verify agreement on the overlap, and identify the unique global regular function they define.

    A hint

    A prime ideal cannot contain both t and 1-t. Simplify each section in its localization, then use the sheaf gluing axiom.

    Work through the solution

    A point of XXX is a prime ideal of k[t]k[t]k[t]. If it were outside both D(t)D(t)D(t) and D(1−t)D(1-t)D(1−t), it would contain both ttt and 1−t1-t1−t, hence their sum 111, which is impossible. Thus U∪V=XU\cup V=XU∪V=X, and U∩V=D(t(1−t))U\cap V=D(t(1-t))U∩V=D(t(1−t)).

    In k[t]tk[t]_tk[t]t​, sU=t2/t=ts_U=t^2/t=tsU​=t2/t=t. In k[t]1−tk[t]_{1-t}k[t]1−t​, sV=t(1−t)/(1−t)=ts_V=t(1-t)/(1-t)=tsV​=t(1−t)/(1−t)=t. The structure sheaf gluing axiom gives a unique global section, explicitly the polynomial t∈k[t]=Γ(X,OX)t\in k[t]=\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)t∈k[t]=Γ(X,OX​). Already comfortable with this? Skip affine-variety review if you can do both checks and explain the reversed direction of maps on coordinate rings. Keep localization beside the geometry

The useful test here is not how many definitions you can repeat, but whether a fraction in a ring tells you where a function is defined. Keep one affine line in view while reading about sheaves.

What to read for this step

Milne remains the starting text for the variety route: chapter 2, sections a–e and h–j, with chapter 4, sections a and d for tangent spaces. Use the existing chapter exercises rather than reading every preliminary chapter again.

For the scheme continuation, use Vakil’s October 21, 2025 draft: §§2.1–2.4 for sheaves and stalks, §§3.2–3.5 for spectra and topology, then §§4.1–4.3 for the structure sheaf and schemes. The route introduction cites the November 18, 2017 early, out-of-date draft. These are different files: the 2025 section suggestions here do not replace or renumber that citation.

When to review, when to continue

If the fractions and the cover argument are routine, skip this warm-up and work on stalks or the structure sheaf. If you can compute tangent spaces but cannot restrict a section, review localization and sheaf gluing first; solving the cusp check alone does not settle that gap.

Editorial choice and revision

Milne supplies concrete variety calculations; the selected Vakil sections explain the passage to schemes. They serve different gaps, so neither book is assigned cover to cover.
