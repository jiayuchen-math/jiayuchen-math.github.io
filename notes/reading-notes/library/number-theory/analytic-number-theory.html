Studying Analytic Number Theory

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Analytic number theory combines arithmetic structure with estimates, so manipulating a Dirichlet series is only the beginning of an argument. Kedlaya organizes a route through multiplicative functions, Euler products and complex methods for prime distribution. Richert develops sieve techniques as a focused branch. Choose the first route while learning which sums and products may be rearranged; choose the sieve continuation after asymptotic estimates have become dependable.

What to work on

  • Justify an Euler product in a half-plane of absolute convergence.
  • Use Dirichlet convolution and Mobius inversion.
  • Identify the Dirichlet series of divisor and von Mangoldt functions.
  • Distinguish the analytic input for prime-number and arithmetic-progression theorems.

A starting text

Notes on Analytic Number Theory — Kiran S. Kedlaya. Begin with 2.1-2.3 on Dirichlet series, multiplicativity and Euler products. Then read 1.2-1.4 on zeta and the Tauberian route to the prime number theorem; 3.1-3.4 and 4.1-4.3 introduce characters, L-functions and primes in progressions.

Choosing what to read

Prove an arithmetic-function identity by counting divisor pairs, then justify the corresponding Dirichlet-series identity in a region of absolute convergence. Write error terms with the variable and constants they depend on. Use the hyperbola method on a divisor sum to see how a geometric decomposition produces an estimate. Before reading a prime-number theorem proof, separate its analytic input from its arithmetic conclusion. For sieve arguments, identify the set being sifted and the local exclusions before handling weights.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. For complex s with real part greater than 1, prove that the Dirichlet series of the divisor-counting function d(n) equals zeta(s) squared. Justify rearranging the double sum.

    A hint

    Each positive divisor corresponds to an ordered pair ab=n. Bound the absolute double sum at the real part of s.

    If this was the sticking point:

    • Real Analysis — Review absolute convergence and rearrangements of infinite sums.
    • Elementary Number Theory — Review the divisor function and divisor-pair counting.

Already comfortable with this? Skip arithmetic-function review if you can derive both identities and control convergence. Continue with the zeta and L-function readings if analytic continuation, nonvanishing and Tauberian hypotheses are still gaps; a formal Euler-product expansion alone does not justify the prime number theorem.
