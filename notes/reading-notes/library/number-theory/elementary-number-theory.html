Studying Elementary Number Theory

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Elementary number theory turns experiments with integers into proofs about divisibility and congruences. Stein develops that route with Euclidean algorithms, modular arithmetic and quadratic reciprocity. Shoup connects integer and finite-field arithmetic to algorithms and cryptography. Choose the first route to strengthen proof and congruence skills; choose the computational continuation when you can explain an algorithm's correctness as well as produce its output.

What to work on

  • Use the Euclidean algorithm and Bezout's identity.
  • Solve congruences and apply the Chinese remainder theorem.
  • Compute modular inverses and powers with stated coprimality conditions.
  • Use quadratic reciprocity and Euler's criterion.

A starting text

Elementary Number Theory: Primes, Congruences, and Secrets — William Stein. Read 1.1 on factorization and the Euclidean algorithm; 2.1-2.3 on congruences, the Chinese remainder theorem, inverses and powers; then 4.1-4.3 on quadratic reciprocity and Euler's criterion.

Choosing what to read

Run the extended Euclidean algorithm by hand and interpret its coefficients as a proof of a modular inverse. Solve a Chinese remainder problem, then verify the solution in every modulus. List small quadratic residues before applying reciprocity, keeping track of the parity signs. For an algorithmic example, separate correctness, termination and cost. Test a calculation on small inputs, but explain why the proof covers all permitted inputs rather than using numerical evidence as the final argument.

  1. Use the extended Euclidean algorithm to find the inverse of 17 modulo 43, and solve x=2 modulo 3, x=3 modulo 5 and x=2 modulo 7, with a uniqueness statement.

    A hint

    Back-substitute the Euclidean divisions. Combine the congruences modulo 3 and 7 first.

    Work through the solution
