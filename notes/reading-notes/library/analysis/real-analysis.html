Studying Real Analysis

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

The first analysis course asks you to justify operations that calculus made familiar: taking limits, passing a limit through a function, and integrating a sequence. Lebl organizes an entry through sequences and continuity before uniform convergence. Trench revisits calculus with detailed theorem statements and worked examples, placing sequences later. Choose the order that exposes your actual difficulty rather than repeating every computational topic.

What to work on

  • Write epsilon proofs for limits of sequences.
  • Use completeness to prove convergence of monotone bounded or Cauchy sequences.
  • Distinguish continuity from uniform continuity by examples.
  • Test uniform convergence before interchanging limits, integrals or derivatives.

A starting text

Basic Analysis I: Introduction to Real Analysis, Volume I — Jiří Lebl. Read sections 2.1-2.4 on sequences and completeness, 3.2-3.4 on continuity, and 6.1-6.2 on uniform convergence and interchange of limits. Review 1.2 on the real numbers if the least-upper-bound property is unfamiliar.

Choosing what to read

For each convergence theorem, write its quantifiers and one example where a missing hypothesis breaks the conclusion. Compare pointwise convergence of x^n on [0,1] with uniform convergence on [0,a] for a<1. Attempt an epsilon proof before consulting an example, then revise the argument so its bound depends only on the permitted variables. Bring completeness into the proof explicitly when a supremum or a convergent subsequence is used.
