Studying Calculus

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Calculus links local rates of change to accumulated change, and the first difficulty is often choosing a model rather than carrying out a derivative. CLP-1 concentrates on differential calculus with explicit worked examples. Active Calculus asks readers to develop ideas through guided activities and includes an integral-calculus route. Choose the former for a focused repair of limits and differentiation; choose the latter when explaining graphs and models needs as much attention as symbolic manipulation.

What to work on

  • Compute a derivative from its defining limit and interpret it as a rate.
  • Use chain and implicit differentiation in an equation with a stated domain.
  • Find and justify a constrained one-variable optimum, including endpoints.
  • Use the fundamental theorem of calculus to connect accumulation and differentiation.

A starting text

CLP-1 Differential Calculus — Joel Feldman, Andrew Rechnitzer and Elyse Yeager. Read sections 1.3 and 1.6 on limits and continuity; 2.2-2.3 on the derivative; 2.9 and 2.11 on chain and implicit rules; and 3.5 on optimisation. Use worked examples to identify domains and endpoint checks.

Choosing what to read

Sketch the function and state its domain before calculating. Compare a derivative obtained from the definition with one obtained by rules, and explain the sign in context. In optimization, list endpoints and points where differentiability fails as well as critical points. Work a problem without looking at its hint, then use the hint to identify the missing decision. Keep integral and series preparation on your plan if the next goal is real analysis.
