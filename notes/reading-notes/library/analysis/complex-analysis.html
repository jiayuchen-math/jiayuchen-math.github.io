Studying Complex Analysis & Several Complex Variables

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Complex differentiability imposes much stronger structure than real differentiability, which is why local hypotheses can lead to contour formulas and global restrictions. Lebl develops that chain through holomorphic functions, Cauchy's theorem, Laurent series and residues. Beck and collaborators offer a concise first-course presentation of holomorphic functions and contour methods. Use the second account to compare a difficult argument, while keeping one consistent set of conventions for contours and singularities.

What to work on

  • Use complex differentiability and Cauchy-Riemann equations with the required regularity hypotheses.
  • Apply Cauchy's formula to obtain derivatives and estimates.
  • Classify isolated singularities and compute residues.
  • Compare zero sets and holomorphic extension in one and several complex variables.

A starting text

Guide to Cultivating Complex Analysis: Working the Complex Field — Jiří Lebl. Read 2.1 on holomorphic functions, 3.2-3.3 on Cauchy's theorem and consequences, 4.4 on Laurent series, and 5.1-5.4 on zeros, singularities and residues. Review line integrals in 3.1 if needed.

Choosing what to read

Check complex differentiability from the definition before relying on the Cauchy-Riemann equations. Draw the contour and mark its interior singularities before selecting a theorem. Expand a function on two different annuli to see why the domain belongs to a Laurent series statement. In a residue calculation, separate the local coefficient calculation from the global contour argument. Record exactly where holomorphicity and the geometry of the domain enter each proof.
