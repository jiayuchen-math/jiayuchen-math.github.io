Studying Differential & Riemannian Geometry

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Classical differential geometry relates derivatives to the shape of curves and surfaces. Shifrin develops arclength, moving frames, fundamental forms and geodesics with calculations and selected assistance for exercises. Petrunin and Zamora Barrera take a visual, problem-centered route through the same classical setting. Choose according to whether your main difficulty is carrying out the local calculation or understanding the geometric claim it expresses.

What to work on

  • Reparametrize a regular curve by arclength and compute curvature and torsion.
  • Compute first and second fundamental forms of a regular surface.
  • Relate principal curvatures to Gaussian and mean curvature.
  • Distinguish intrinsic surface geometry from features of an embedding.

A starting text

Differential Geometry: A First Course in Curves and Surfaces — Theodore Shifrin. Read chapter 1, sections 1-2 on arclength and Frenet frames; chapter 2, sections 1-2 on the fundamental forms and Gauss map; then chapter 2, section 4 on covariant differentiation and geodesics. Chapter 3, section 1 introduces holonomy and Gauss-Bonnet.

Choosing what to read

Parametrize a helix by arclength and compute its curvature before reading the general frame formulas. On a sphere and a cylinder, calculate the fundamental forms and Gaussian curvature, tracking the chosen normal and shape-operator convention. Compare a curve's ambient acceleration with its covariant derivative on the surface. Draw the geometry, then annotate the drawing with the quantities you computed. Plan a separate smooth-manifold and tensor course if the next goal requires coordinate-independent constructions in higher dimensions.
