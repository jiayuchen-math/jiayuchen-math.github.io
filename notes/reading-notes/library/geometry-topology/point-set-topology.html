Studying Point-Set Topology

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Topology supplies a language in which continuity, compactness and connectedness survive without a distance formula. Morris develops that language through definitions, examples and exercises. Hatcher provides a shorter bridge toward algebraic topology. Use the longer route when you need practice recognizing topologies and continuous maps; use the shorter account when familiar metric-space intuition needs translation into open-set arguments.

What to work on

  • Prove continuity using inverse images of open sets.
  • Distinguish connectedness, compactness and separation assumptions in examples.
  • Use bases, subspaces and product topologies.
  • Verify the defining property of a quotient topology.

A starting text

Topology Without Tears — Sidney A. Morris. Read 1.1-1.3 on topologies, 2.2-2.3 on bases, 3.1 and 3.3 on closure and connectedness, 4.1 and 5.1 on subspaces and continuity, 7.1-7.2 on compactness, 8.1-8.2 on products, and 11.1 on quotient spaces.

Choosing what to read

Test every definition on a finite space, a metric space and a space with a less familiar topology. Prove continuity once by inverse images of opens and once in a metric example, explaining why sequences are not a universal replacement. For compactness, work directly with an open cover and a finite subcover. When constructing a quotient, describe the quotient map and the topology it induces before drawing a picture. Keep counterexamples close to the separation assumptions they test.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. Prove that a continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism. Give a continuous bijection that fails to be a homeomorphism when these hypotheses are dropped.

    A hint

    Show the bijection is closed. For a counterexample, compare two topologies on the same two-point set.

    Work through the solution

    Let f:X→Yf:X\to Yf:X→Y be a continuous bijection, with XXX compact and YYY Hausdorff. A closed subset C⊆XC\subseteq XC⊆X is compact: append X∖CX\setminus CX∖C to any open cover of CCC and use compactness of XXX. Its image f(C)f(C)f(C) is compact by continuity. A compact subset KKK of a Hausdorff space is closed: for y∉Ky\notin Ky∈/K, separate yyy from each k∈Kk\in Kk∈K by disjoint open sets, cover KKK by finitely many of the neighborhoods of kkk, and intersect the corresponding neighborhoods of yyy. This produces an open neighborhood of yyy disjoint from KKK. Thus fff is closed. For g=f−1g=f^{-1}g=f−1, the preimage g−1(C)=f(C)g^{-1}(C)=f(C)g−1(C)=f(C) of every closed C⊆XC\subseteq XC⊆X is closed in YYY, proving continuity of ggg.

    For a counterexample, let X={0,1}X=\{0,1\}X={0,1} be discrete and let the same underlying set YYY have the indiscrete topology {∅,Y}\{\varnothing,Y\}{∅,Y}. The identity map X→YX\to YX→Y is a continuous bijection. Its inverse is not continuous, because the inverse image of the open singleton {0}⊆X\{0\}\subseteq X{0}⊆X is not open in YYY. Here XXX is compact, but YYY is not Hausdorff, so the theorem's hypotheses are not all present.

    If this was the sticking point:

    • Proofs & Mathematical Writing — Review inverse-image tests for continuity and finite-subcover arguments.
    • Set Theory — Review the difference between a set bijection and additional structure on the sets.
