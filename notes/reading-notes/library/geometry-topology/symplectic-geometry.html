Studying Symplectic Geometry

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

A symplectic form pairs tangent directions in a way that leads naturally to Hamiltonian motion and Lagrangian submanifolds. Cannas da Silva's shorter IMPA course provides a route through local normal forms, cotangent bundles and Hamiltonian fields. Calegari's notes emphasize a topological continuation toward holomorphic curves and an outline of Floer homology. The latter needs geometric and analytic preparation beyond understanding Hamilton's equations in coordinates.

What to work on

  • Test closedness and nondegeneracy of a two-form.
  • Use Darboux's theorem with its local scope.
  • Compute Hamiltonian vector fields and identify their conserved energy.
  • Check when a graph in a cotangent bundle is Lagrangian.

A starting text

Introduction to Symplectic and Hamiltonian Geometry — Ana Cannas da Silva. Read 1.1-1.4 for linear algebra and symplectic manifolds, 1.7-1.9 for Moser's method and Darboux, 2.1-2.4 for cotangent bundles and Lagrangians, and 4.1-4.3 for Hamiltonian fields, Hamilton equations and brackets. Sections 4.6-4.7 and 5.1-5.3 continue to moment maps and reduction.

Choosing what to read

Compute the Hamiltonian vector field of a quadratic Hamiltonian using the sign convention of the chosen text. Check that the flow preserves the symplectic form, then test when the graph of a one-form is Lagrangian. Read Moser's argument by tracking the time-dependent vector field and the equation it must solve. Before following holomorphic curves or Floer theory, list the required compactness and regularity inputs; a course outline signals these constructions without supplying every foundation.

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. On R squared with omega=dq wedge dp, use the convention contraction of X_H with omega equals dH to compute X_H for H=(q squared+p squared)/2. Solve its flow and verify that H is conserved.

    A hint

    For X=a partial_q+b partial_p, contraction with dq wedge dp equals a dp-b dq. Keep this sign convention throughout.

    Work through the solution

    Write XH=a∂q+b∂pX_H=a\partial_q+b\partial_pXH​=a∂q​+b∂p​. With ω=dq∧dp\omega=dq\wedge dpω=dq∧dp, ιXHω=a dp−b dq\iota_{X_H}\omega=a\,dp-b\,dqιXH​​ω=adp−bdq. Equating this to dH=q dq+p dpdH=q\,dq+p\,dpdH=qdq+pdp gives a=pa=pa=p, b=−qb=-qb=−q, so XH=p∂q−q∂pX_H=p\partial_q-q\partial_pXH​=p∂q​−q∂p​. The equations are q′=pq'=pq′=p, p′=−qp'=-qp′=−q.

    For initial data (q0,p0)(q_0,p_0)(q0​,p0​), the global solution is q(t)=q0cos⁡t+p0sin⁡tq(t)=q_0\cos t+p_0\sin tq(t)=q0​cost+p0​sint and p(t)=−q0sin⁡t+p0cos⁡tp(t)=-q_0\sin t+p_0\cos tp(t)=−q0​sint+p0​cost. Differentiation verifies both equations and evaluation at t=0t=0t=0 verifies the initial data. The flow is clockwise rotation in the (q,p)(q,p)(q,p) drawing plane. Along it, dH/dt=qq′+pp′=qp−pq=0dH/dt=qq'+pp'=qp-pq=0dH/dt=qq′+pp′=qp−pq=0, so HHH is constant. Choosing the opposite contraction convention would reverse the direction, which is why the convention is part of the question.

    If this was the sticking point:

    • Differential & Riemannian Geometry — Review differential forms, wedge products and contraction.
    • Differential Equations & PDEs — Review linear systems and verifying an explicit flow.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the first lecture only if differential forms and symplectic linear algebra are usable in proofs. Skip the selection if both checks are routine and you can explain why Darboux gives local equivalence without implying global symplectomorphism.
